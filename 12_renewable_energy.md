## 12.1 Renewable Energy: Basic Concepts

**Renewable energy** is energy obtained from natural sources that are replenished on a human timescale.

### Classification of Energy Sources

| Renewable Energy | Non-Renewable Energy |
|---|---|
| Solar | Coal |
| Wind | Petroleum |
| Hydropower | Natural gas |
| Biomass | Uranium |
| Geothermal | — |
| Tidal and wave energy | — |
| Ocean Thermal Energy Conversion | — |

### Important Distinctions

- **Renewable energy:** Energy from naturally replenished sources.
- **Non-fossil energy:** Includes renewable energy and nuclear energy.
- **Clean energy:** A broader term generally referring to energy sources with relatively low pollution or emissions.
- **Green energy:** Commonly used for energy sources with comparatively low environmental impacts; its precise scope varies.

**UPSC Trap:** Nuclear energy is non-fossil but is not conventionally classified as renewable energy.

# 12.2 Solar Energy

Solar energy can be converted into electricity or heat.

### Major Technologies

| Technology | Principle |
|---|---|
| Solar Photovoltaic (PV) | Direct conversion of sunlight into electricity |
| Concentrated Solar Power (CSP) | Concentration of sunlight to generate heat, which can be used to produce electricity |
| Solar Thermal Collector | Conversion of sunlight into useful heat |
| Solar Water Heater | Uses solar energy to heat water |
| Solar Cooker | Uses solar radiation for cooking |

## Solar Photovoltaic Cells

### Working Principle

Solar PV cells operate through the **photovoltaic effect**.

1. Photons are absorbed by a semiconductor.
2. Electron–hole pairs are generated.
3. An internal electric field separates charge carriers.
4. Electric current is produced through an external circuit.

### Important Facts

- Silicon is the most widely used semiconductor in commercial solar cells.
- Solar PV cells generate **direct current (DC)**.
- An inverter converts DC into alternating current (AC).
- Solar PV cells can generate electricity from diffuse sunlight, although output is lower under reduced irradiance.

### Important Solar Cell Technologies

| Technology | Important Feature |
|---|---|
| Monocrystalline silicon | Manufactured from single-crystal silicon |
| Polycrystalline silicon | Manufactured from multicrystalline silicon |
| Thin-film solar cells | Thin semiconductor layers |
| Perovskite solar cells | Use perovskite-structured light-absorbing materials |
| Tandem solar cells | Combine multiple light-absorbing junctions |

**Important:** Perovskite solar cells are an emerging technology; stability and large-scale commercialization remain important considerations.

# 12.3 Concentrated Solar Power (CSP)

CSP uses mirrors or other optical systems to concentrate sunlight and produce high-temperature heat.

### Important Technologies

- Parabolic trough.
- Solar power tower.
- Linear Fresnel reflector.
- Parabolic dish.

### Solar PV vs CSP

| Solar PV | CSP |
|---|---|
| Direct conversion of sunlight into electricity | Converts concentrated sunlight into heat |
| Uses semiconductor cells | Uses mirrors or optical concentrators |
| Can use direct and diffuse sunlight | Primarily requires direct normal solar radiation |
| Electricity storage generally requires a separate system | Can integrate thermal energy storage |

### Important Fact

Molten salts can be used for thermal energy storage in CSP plants.

**UPSC Trap:** CSP and solar PV do not operate on the same physical principle.

# 12.5 Wind Energy

Wind turbines convert the kinetic energy of moving air into mechanical energy and subsequently electricity.

### Important Components

- Rotor blades.
- Hub.
- Generator.
- Tower.
- Control systems.
- Power electronics.

### Types of Wind Turbines

| Type | Important Feature |
|---|---|
| Horizontal-Axis Wind Turbine (HAWT) | Rotor axis generally parallel to wind direction |
| Vertical-Axis Wind Turbine (VAWT) | Rotor axis vertical |

### Onshore vs Offshore Wind Energy

| Onshore | Offshore |
|---|---|
| Installed on land | Installed in water bodies, usually seas |
| Generally lower installation costs | Generally higher installation and maintenance costs |
| Land availability and local wind conditions are important | Often benefits from stronger and more consistent winds |

### Important Facts

- Wind power output depends strongly on wind speed.
- The theoretical maximum fraction of wind power extractable by an ideal turbine is approximately **59.3%**, known as the **Betz limit**.
- Actual turbine efficiency is lower because of aerodynamic, mechanical and electrical losses.

### India's Wind Energy

- Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Karnataka, Maharashtra and Rajasthan are important wind-energy states.
- Offshore wind potential exists along India's coastline, particularly near Gujarat and Tamil Nadu.

# 12.6 Hydropower

Hydropower converts the potential or kinetic energy of water into electricity.

### Important Types

| Type | Important Feature |
|---|---|
| Storage hydropower | Uses water stored in a reservoir |
| Run-of-river hydropower | Uses river flow, sometimes with limited storage |
| Pumped-storage hydropower | Stores energy by pumping water to a higher reservoir |
| Small hydropower | In India, projects up to 25 MW |

### Pumped-Storage Hydropower

#### Working

1. During periods of surplus electricity, water is pumped to an upper reservoir.
2. During periods of high demand, water flows to a lower reservoir through turbines.
3. Electricity is generated.

### Important Facts

- Pumped storage is primarily an **energy-storage technology**.
- It consumes more electricity during pumping than it generates during discharge because of losses.
- It helps balance variable renewable electricity generation.

### Classification in India

| Category | Capacity |
|---|---|
| Small Hydropower | Up to 25 MW |
| Large Hydropower | Above 25 MW |

**Important:** India recognizes large hydropower projects as renewable-energy sources.

# 12.7 Biomass Energy

Biomass consists of organic materials derived from plants, animals and biological wastes.

### Important Sources

- Agricultural residues.
- Forestry residues.
- Animal dung.
- Food waste.
- Organic municipal waste.
- Energy crops.

### Major Conversion Technologies

| Technology | Process | Product |
|---|---|---|
| Direct combustion | Burning biomass | Heat/electricity |
| Anaerobic digestion | Microbial decomposition without oxygen | Biogas |
| Gasification | Partial oxidation | Producer gas/syngas |
| Pyrolysis | Thermal decomposition without oxygen | Bio-oil, gas and char |
| Fermentation | Microbial conversion of sugars | Bioethanol |
| Transesterification | Chemical conversion of oils and fats | Biodiesel |

### Important Facts

- Biomass is renewable when managed sustainably.
- Biomass combustion can produce particulate matter and other air pollutants.
- Biomass energy is not automatically carbon-neutral when lifecycle emissions and land-use changes are considered.

# 12.8 Biogas and Compressed Biogas

## Biogas

Produced through **anaerobic digestion** of biodegradable organic matter.

### Main Components

- Methane (CH₄).
- Carbon dioxide (CO₂).
- Small amounts of other gases.

### Important Feedstocks

- Cattle dung.
- Agricultural residues.
- Food waste.
- Sewage sludge.
- Other biodegradable organic waste.

## Compressed Biogas (CBG)

Biogas is purified to increase methane concentration and then compressed for use as fuel.

### Biogas vs CBG

| Biogas | Compressed Biogas |
|---|---|
| Contains substantial CO₂ | CO₂ and impurities are substantially removed |
| Generally lower methane concentration | Higher methane concentration |
| Used for cooking, heating and electricity | Can be used as a transport or industrial fuel |

### SATAT Initiative

**SATAT:** Sustainable Alternative Towards Affordable Transportation.

- Launched in **2018**.
- Promotes compressed biogas production from biomass and waste.

### GOBARdhan Initiative

Promotes conversion of cattle dung and other biodegradable waste into useful products such as biogas, CBG and organic manure.

# 12.9 Biofuels

Biofuels are fuels produced from biological materials.

### Major Biofuels

| Biofuel | Important Feedstock |
|---|---|
| Bioethanol | Sugarcane, maize, other suitable biomass |
| Biodiesel | Vegetable oils, used cooking oil, animal fats |
| Biogas | Biodegradable organic waste |
| Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF) | Eligible biomass, waste oils and other qualifying feedstocks |

## Biofuel Generations

| Generation | Typical Feedstock |
|---|---|
| First generation | Food-based crops such as sugarcane, maize and vegetable oils |
| Second generation | Agricultural residues and lignocellulosic biomass |
| Third generation | Algae |
| Fourth generation | Advanced approaches involving engineered organisms or carbon-capture integration |

### Important Distinctions

- First-generation biofuels may compete with food production and agricultural land.
- Second-generation biofuels can use agricultural residues.
- Algae are associated with third-generation biofuels.
- The classification of fourth-generation biofuels varies across sources.

# 12.11 Geothermal Energy

Geothermal energy uses heat from within the Earth.

### Important Sources

- Geothermal reservoirs.
- Hot springs.
- Underground hot water and steam.
- Hot dry rock formations.

### Major Technologies

| Technology | Important Feature |
|---|---|
| Dry Steam Plant | Uses naturally occurring steam |
| Flash Steam Plant | Converts high-pressure hot water into steam |
| Binary Cycle Plant | Transfers geothermal heat to a secondary fluid |
| Ground-Source Heat Pump | Uses relatively stable ground temperatures for heating and cooling |

### Important Facts

- Geothermal energy is not dependent on daily sunlight or wind availability.
- Electricity generation generally requires suitable geothermal resources.
- Ground-source heat pumps do not necessarily generate electricity.

### Important Geothermal Regions in India

- Puga Valley, Ladakh.
- Manikaran, Himachal Pradesh.
- Tattapani, Chhattisgarh.
- Cambay Basin, Gujarat.

# 12.12 Ocean Energy

### Major Types

| Technology | Energy Source |
|---|---|
| Tidal Energy | Periodic movement of seawater due to gravitational forces |
| Wave Energy | Energy of surface ocean waves |
| Ocean Thermal Energy Conversion (OTEC) | Temperature difference between warm surface water and cold deep water |
| Ocean Current Energy | Kinetic energy of ocean currents |
| Salinity Gradient Energy | Difference in salt concentration between water bodies |

## Tidal Energy

- Tides are caused primarily by the gravitational attraction of the Moon and Sun.
- Tidal barrages exploit differences in water levels.
- Tidal-stream turbines exploit moving tidal currents.

### Potential Regions in India

- Gulf of Khambhat.
- Gulf of Kachchh.
- Sundarbans region.

## Ocean Thermal Energy Conversion

### Working Principle

OTEC exploits the temperature difference between warm surface seawater and cold deep seawater.

### Important Facts

- Best suited to tropical ocean regions with a sufficiently large vertical temperature difference.
- May use open-cycle, closed-cycle or hybrid systems.
- Closed-cycle OTEC uses a working fluid with a low boiling point.

### Important Distinction

| Tidal Energy | OTEC |
|---|---|
| Depends on tidal movement or water-level differences | Depends on ocean temperature differences |
| Influenced by gravitational forces | Uses ocean thermal gradients |
| Highly predictable tidal cycles | More suitable in tropical oceans |

# 12.13 Hydrogen as an Energy Carrier

Hydrogen is an **energy carrier**, not a primary energy source.

It can be produced using various energy sources and technologies.

## Types of Hydrogen

| Type | Production Method |
|---|---|
| Grey Hydrogen | Generally produced from fossil fuels without carbon capture |
| Blue Hydrogen | Fossil-fuel-based production with carbon capture and storage |
| Green Hydrogen | Produced using renewable energy through processes such as water electrolysis |
| Brown/Black Hydrogen | Commonly associated with coal-based production |
| Pink Hydrogen | Hydrogen produced using nuclear energy |
| Turquoise Hydrogen | Methane pyrolysis producing hydrogen and solid carbon |

**Important:** Hydrogen colour terminology is not completely standardized internationally.

### Important Distinctions

- Hydrogen is not automatically environmentally friendly.
- Its environmental impact depends on production, transport and use.
- Green hydrogen can help decarbonize sectors that are difficult to electrify directly.

# 12.14 Water Electrolysis

Electrolysis uses electrical energy to split water into hydrogen and oxygen.

\[
2H_2O \rightarrow 2H_2 + O_2
\]

### Important Technologies

| Electrolyser | Important Feature |
|---|---|
| Alkaline Electrolyser | Uses an alkaline electrolyte |
| Proton Exchange Membrane (PEM) Electrolyser | Uses a proton-conducting polymer membrane |
| Solid Oxide Electrolyser | Operates at high temperatures |
| Anion Exchange Membrane (AEM) Electrolyser | Uses an anion-conducting membrane |

### Important Facts

- Electrolysis consumes electricity.
- Green hydrogen production generally requires renewable electricity and compliance with applicable emissions criteria.
- Hydrogen can be stored, transported or converted into derivatives such as ammonia.

### Electrolyser vs Fuel Cell

| Electrolyser | Fuel Cell |
|---|---|
| Uses electricity to produce hydrogen | Uses hydrogen or another fuel to produce electricity |
| Converts electrical energy into chemical energy | Converts chemical energy into electrical energy |
| Water splitting | Electrochemical fuel oxidation |

# 12.16 Fuel Cells

A fuel cell converts chemical energy directly into electrical energy through electrochemical reactions.

### Hydrogen Fuel Cell Reaction

\[
2H_2 + O_2 \rightarrow 2H_2O + \text{Energy}
\]

### Important Components

- Anode.
- Cathode.
- Electrolyte.
- External electrical circuit.

### Types of Fuel Cells

| Fuel Cell | Important Feature |
|---|---|
| Proton Exchange Membrane Fuel Cell (PEMFC) | Commonly used in hydrogen fuel-cell vehicles |
| Solid Oxide Fuel Cell (SOFC) | High-temperature operation |
| Alkaline Fuel Cell (AFC) | Uses alkaline electrolyte |
| Phosphoric Acid Fuel Cell (PAFC) | Uses phosphoric acid electrolyte |
| Molten Carbonate Fuel Cell (MCFC) | High-temperature operation |

### Fuel Cell vs Battery

| Fuel Cell | Battery |
|---|---|
| Requires continuous fuel and oxidant supply | Stores electrochemical energy internally |
| Can operate while fuel is supplied | Requires recharging or replacement after discharge |
| Hydrogen fuel cells produce water at the point of use | Products depend on battery chemistry |

**UPSC Trap:** A hydrogen fuel cell does not produce hydrogen; it consumes hydrogen.

# 12.17 Green Ammonia

Ammonia has the chemical formula:

\[
NH_3
\]

### Production

Ammonia is conventionally produced using hydrogen and nitrogen through the Haber–Bosch process.

\[
N_2 + 3H_2 \rightleftharpoons 2NH_3
\]

### Green Ammonia

Generally refers to ammonia produced using low-emission processes, particularly renewable hydrogen.

### Important Uses

- Fertilizers.
- Industrial chemicals.
- Potential energy carrier.
- Potential marine fuel.

### Important Facts

- Ammonia contains hydrogen but no carbon.
- Ammonia combustion does not directly produce CO₂ from the ammonia molecule.
- Nitrogen oxides may be formed during combustion.
- Ammonia is toxic and requires careful handling.

# 12.19 Lithium-Ion Batteries

Lithium-ion batteries store and release energy through the movement of lithium ions between electrodes.

### Important Components

- Cathode.
- Anode.
- Electrolyte.
- Separator.
- Current collectors.

### Important Battery Chemistries

| Chemistry | Important Elements |
|---|---|
| LFP | Lithium, iron and phosphate |
| NMC | Lithium, nickel, manganese and cobalt |
| NCA | Lithium, nickel, cobalt and aluminium |
| LCO | Lithium and cobalt |

### Important Facts

- Lithium-ion batteries generally do not use metallic lithium as the anode in conventional commercial designs.
- Graphite is a common anode material.
- Lithium-ion batteries can undergo thermal runaway under certain failure conditions.

### LFP vs NMC

| LFP | NMC |
|---|---|
| Lithium iron phosphate | Lithium nickel manganese cobalt oxide |
| Does not require nickel or cobalt in the cathode | Uses nickel, manganese and cobalt |
| Generally better thermal stability | Often higher energy density |

# 12.20 Sodium-Ion Batteries

### Important Facts

- Use sodium ions as charge carriers.
- Sodium is relatively abundant.
- Do not inherently require lithium.
- Can use various cathode and anode materials.

### Sodium-Ion vs Lithium-Ion

| Sodium-Ion | Lithium-Ion |
|---|---|
| Sodium-based chemistry | Lithium-based chemistry |
| Sodium is relatively abundant | Lithium resources are more geographically concentrated |
| Often lower energy density in current commercial technologies | Generally higher energy density |
| Emerging commercial applications | Widely commercialized |

**Important:** Sodium-ion batteries are rechargeable electrochemical batteries.

# 12.21 Solid-State Batteries

### Important Features

- Use a solid electrolyte instead of a conventional liquid electrolyte.
- May offer improvements in safety and energy density.
- Can use different electrode chemistries.

### Important Facts

- Solid-state batteries are not necessarily lithium-free.
- Solid electrolytes can be ceramic, polymeric or composite materials.
- Commercial scalability and interface stability remain important technological challenges.

# 12.25 Carbon Capture, Utilization and Storage (CCUS)

CCUS involves capturing carbon dioxide from industrial or energy-related sources and using or storing it.

### Important Technologies

| Technology | Important Feature |
|---|---|
| Post-combustion capture | Captures CO₂ after fuel combustion |
| Pre-combustion capture | Separates CO₂ before final fuel combustion |
| Oxy-fuel combustion | Uses oxygen-enriched combustion to facilitate CO₂ capture |
| Direct Air Capture (DAC) | Captures CO₂ directly from ambient air |
| Geological storage | Stores CO₂ in suitable geological formations |

### Important Facts

- CCS can reduce emissions from industrial facilities.
- DAC removes CO₂ from the atmosphere.
- Capturing CO₂ from fossil-fuel combustion does not automatically constitute atmospheric carbon removal.
- CO₂ utilization does not always ensure permanent storage.

### Major Technologies

| Technology | Principle |

# 12.32 Important UPSC Prelims Distinctions

| Concept A | Concept B | Key Difference |
|---|---|---|
| Renewable energy | Non-fossil energy | Non-fossil also includes nuclear |
| Solar PV | CSP | Photovoltaic conversion vs concentrated solar heat |
