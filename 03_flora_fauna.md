3. Black-necked Crane

**[CSE 2014]**

**Answer: B. 2 only**

>**Explanation:**
>
>**1. Painted Stork — Incorrect**
>
>- Primarily feeds on aquatic animals, especially fish.
>- Usually forages in shallow wetlands.
>- It is not characteristically associated with capturing insects disturbed by grazing cattle.
>
>**2. Common Myna — Correct**
>
>- Common Mynas frequently forage on the ground.
>- They consume insects disturbed by grazing cattle and other large animals.
>- This is a well-known feeding association.
>
>**3. Black-necked Crane — Incorrect**
>
>- Primarily inhabits high-altitude wetlands and surrounding habitats.
>- Feeds on plant material and various small animals.
>- It is not characteristically known for following grazing cattle to capture disturbed insects.
>
>**Important concepts:**
>
>This association is commonly described as **commensalism**.
>
>- The Common Myna benefits by obtaining insects.
>- The cattle are generally neither significantly benefited nor harmed by the bird's feeding activity.
>
>**Ecological Interactions**
>
>| Interaction | Species A | Species B | Example |
>|---|---|---|---|
>| Mutualism | Benefits | Benefits | Fig and pollinating fig wasp |
>| Commensalism | Benefits | Unaffected | Common Myna feeding on insects disturbed by cattle |
>| Parasitism | Benefits | Harmed | Tapeworm and host |
>| Predation | Benefits | Harmed | Tiger and deer |
>| Competition | Negatively affected | Negatively affected | Plants competing for limited resources |
>
>**Prelims fact:** Commensalism is represented as **(+/0)**, indicating that one species benefits while the other is essentially unaffected.




## 3.1 Introduction

**Flora** refers to the plant life of a particular region, habitat or geological period.

**Fauna** refers to the animal life of a particular region, habitat or geological period.

### Important Concepts

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Flora | Plant life of a region | Rhododendrons of the Himalayas |
| Fauna | Animal life of a region | Snow leopard of the Himalayas |
| Native species | Species occurring naturally in a region | Indian elephant in India |
| Endemic species | Species naturally restricted to a particular geographical region | Lion-tailed macaque in the Western Ghats |
| Exotic species | Species introduced outside its natural range | Water hyacinth in India |
| Invasive alien species | Introduced species that spreads and adversely affects biodiversity | Lantana camara |
| Migratory species | Species undertaking regular seasonal movements | Amur falcon |
| Threatened species | Species classified as Vulnerable, Endangered or Critically Endangered by the IUCN | Tiger, Asian elephant, gharial |

**Important:** A species can be native without being endemic. For example, the Asian elephant is native to India but also occurs naturally in other Asian countries.

# 3.2 Conservation Status of Species

The **International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN)** assesses the extinction risk of species through the IUCN Red List.

### IUCN Red List Categories

| Category | Abbreviation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Extinct | EX | No reasonable doubt that the last individual has died |
| Extinct in the Wild | EW | Survives only in captivity, cultivation or outside its historical natural range |
| Critically Endangered | CR | Extremely high risk of extinction in the wild |
| Endangered | EN | Very high risk of extinction in the wild |
| Vulnerable | VU | High risk of extinction in the wild |
| Near Threatened | NT | Close to qualifying for a threatened category |
| Least Concern | LC | Does not qualify for a higher-risk category |
| Data Deficient | DD | Insufficient information to assess extinction risk |
| Not Evaluated | NE | Not yet assessed against IUCN criteria |

### Important Distinctions

- **Threatened categories:** CR, EN and VU.
- **Near Threatened** is not included among the three threatened categories.
- **Data Deficient** does not mean the species is safe.
- IUCN conservation status is different from legal protection under India's Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972.
- IUCN categories apply to the taxon being assessed; global and regional assessments may differ.

### Great Indian Bustard

- Scientific name: *Ardeotis nigriceps*.
- IUCN status: **Critically Endangered**.
- Primarily associated with open grasslands and semi-arid landscapes.
- Rajasthan is its most important remaining stronghold.
- Desert National Park is an important habitat.
- Threats include habitat loss, fragmentation and collisions with overhead power lines.

**Important:** The Great Indian Bustard is a grassland bird, not a forest bird.

### Vultures and Diclofenac

- Veterinary use of **diclofenac** contributed to severe declines in several South Asian vulture populations.
- Vultures are exposed when they consume carcasses of livestock treated with the drug.
- Diclofenac can cause kidney failure in susceptible vultures.
- Veterinary diclofenac was banned in India in **2006**.

**Important:** Diclofenac is a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID).

# 3.6 Reptiles of India

## A. Crocodilians

India has **three native crocodilian species**.

| Species | Scientific Name | IUCN Status | Important Distribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gharial | *Gavialis gangeticus* | CR | Northern Indian river systems |
| Mugger crocodile | *Crocodylus palustris* | VU | Rivers, lakes, reservoirs and marshes |
| Saltwater crocodile | *Crocodylus porosus* | LC | Coastal and estuarine habitats, including Odisha and Andaman and Nicobar Islands |

### Gharial

- Characterized by a long, narrow snout.
- Primarily fish-eating.
- Associated with large river systems.
- Important habitat: **National Chambal Sanctuary**.

### Mugger Crocodile

- Also called the marsh crocodile.
- Inhabits freshwater habitats.
- Has a relatively broad snout.

### Saltwater Crocodile

- Largest living reptile species.
- Found in coastal and estuarine habitats.
- Important Indian habitats:
  - Bhitarkanika, Odisha.
  - Andaman and Nicobar Islands.

### Ganges River Dolphin

- India's **National Aquatic Animal**, declared in **2009**.
- Found in freshwater river systems.
- Uses echolocation for navigation and feeding.
- Has very limited functional eyesight.
- Threatened by river fragmentation, pollution, fishing-net entanglement and habitat degradation.

**Important:** The Ganges river dolphin is a mammal, not a fish.

### Dugong

- Also called the **sea cow**.
- A marine herbivorous mammal.
- Feeds primarily on seagrasses.
- Important Indian habitats:
  - Gulf of Mannar.
  - Palk Bay.
  - Andaman and Nicobar Islands.
  - Gulf of Kutch, where occurrences are rare.

**Prelims Trap:** Dugongs are associated with **seagrass meadows**, not coral polyps as their principal food.

### Corals

- Corals are **animals**, not plants.
- Reef-building corals commonly have symbiotic photosynthetic dinoflagellates.

- Coral reefs support high marine biodiversity.

- Not a true crab.
- More closely related to arachnids than to true crabs.
- Sometimes called a living fossil.
- Important species along India's eastern coast include *Tachypleus gigas* and *Carcinoscorpius rotundicauda*.

## C. Mangrove Flora

Mangroves are salt-tolerant trees and shrubs growing in tropical and subtropical intertidal environments.

### Important Mangrove Genera

| Genus | Important Feature |
|---|---|
| Rhizophora | Prop or stilt roots |
| Avicennia | Pneumatophores; salt-excreting adaptations |
| Sonneratia | Pneumatophores |
| Bruguiera | Knee-root adaptations in some species |
| Ceriops | Mangrove species adapted to intertidal habitats |
| Heritiera | Includes the sundari tree |

### Sundari Tree

- Scientific name: *Heritiera fomes*.
- Associated with the Sundarbans.
- The Sundarbans name is commonly linked to the sundari tree.

### Mangrove Adaptations

1. Salt exclusion or salt excretion.
2. Aerial roots for gas exchange.
3. Prop or stilt roots for support.
4. Vivipary or cryptovivipary in several species.
5. Adaptations to waterlogged, oxygen-poor soils.

**Important:** Not all mangrove species possess every adaptation.

### Water Hyacinth

- Formerly classified as *Eichhornia crassipes*; currently often treated as *Pontederia crassipes*.
- Native to tropical South America.
- Invasive in Indian freshwater ecosystems.
- Can obstruct waterways and reduce light penetration.
- Dense growth can contribute to oxygen depletion in water bodies.

### Parthenium

- Native to the Americas.
- Invades agricultural fields, grasslands and disturbed lands.
- Produces large quantities of seeds.
- Can cause allergic dermatitis and respiratory problems.

# 3.12 Important Plant Adaptations

| Plant Type | Habitat | Adaptations |
|---|---|---|
| Xerophytes | Dry environments | Reduced leaves, thick cuticle, water storage |
| Hydrophytes | Aquatic environments | Aerenchyma, reduced supporting tissues in many species |
| Halophytes | Saline environments | Salt tolerance, salt exclusion or excretion |
| Epiphytes | Grow on other plants | Aerial roots and adaptations for moisture absorption |
| Lithophytes | Rocky substrates | Root adaptations for rock surfaces |
| Insectivorous plants | Often nutrient-poor habitats | Capture and digest small animals |

### Important Examples

| Plant | Category |
|---|---|
| Cactus | Xerophyte |
| Lotus | Rooted aquatic plant |
| Hydrilla | Submerged aquatic plant |
| Mangroves | Halophytes |
| Orchid | Many species are epiphytes |
| Nepenthes | Carnivorous pitcher plant |
| Drosera | Carnivorous sundew |
| Utricularia | Carnivorous bladderwort |

### Carnivorous Plants

Carnivorous plants generally:
- Perform photosynthesis.
- Capture prey mainly to obtain nutrients, especially nitrogen and phosphorus.
- Occur frequently in nutrient-poor habitats.

**Prelims Trap:** Carnivorous plants are not necessarily heterotrophs for their carbon requirements. Most obtain their carbon primarily through photosynthesis.
