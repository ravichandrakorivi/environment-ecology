# 11. International Climate Agreements and Institutions

## 11.1 Major International Climate Agreements

| Agreement        | Adopted | Main purpose                                                                                                     |
| ---------------- | ------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| UNFCCC           | 1992    | Establishes the international framework for addressing climate change.                                           |
| Kyoto Protocol   | 1997    | Establishes legally binding emission-reduction commitments for specified developed countries.                    |
| Paris Agreement  | 2015    | Establishes global temperature goals and a framework for countries to submit and strengthen climate commitments. |
| Kigali Amendment | 2016    | Provides for the phasedown of hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs) under the Montreal Protocol.                             |

## 11.2 United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC)

### 11.2.1 Establishment and Objective

- Full form: United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change.
- Adopted: 1992; opened for signature at the Rio Earth Summit, Brazil.
- Entered into force: 21 March 1994.
- Nature: International environmental treaty.

Objective: Stabilise atmospheric greenhouse gas concentrations at a level that prevents dangerous human interference with the climate system.

### 11.2.2 Important Principles

Common but Differentiated Responsibilities and Respective Capabilities (CBDR-RC)

- All countries share responsibility for addressing climate change.
- Responsibilities differ according to countries' circumstances, capabilities and contributions to the problem.
- Developed countries are expected to take the lead in combating climate change.

### 11.2.3 Conference of the Parties (COP)

- COP: Conference of the Parties.
- It is the supreme decision-making body of the UNFCCC.
- Comprises countries that are Parties to the Convention.
- Reviews implementation and adopts decisions relating to international climate cooperation.
- COP1: Berlin, Germany, 1995.

Important distinction: The UNFCCC is the treaty, whereas COP is the meeting and decision-making body of its Parties.

## 11.3 Kyoto Protocol

### 11.3.1 Establishment and Objective

- Adopted: 11 December 1997, at COP3 in Kyoto, Japan.
- Entered into force: 16 February 2005.
- Parent convention: UNFCCC.
- Objective: Establish legally binding greenhouse gas emission-reduction commitments for specified developed countries.

The Kyoto Protocol primarily placed quantified emission-reduction obligations on developed countries and economies in transition listed in its Annex B.

Developing countries, including India, did not have binding quantified emission-reduction targets under the Protocol.

### 11.3.2 Commitment Periods

| Commitment period | Years     | Important fact                                           |
| ----------------- | --------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| First             | 2008–2012 | Original binding emission-reduction commitments.         |
| Second            | 2013–2020 | Established through the Doha Amendment, adopted in 2012. |

- Doha Amendment: Entered into force on 31 December 2020.
- The second commitment period applied to participating Parties with commitments under the amendment, not automatically to every developed country.

### 11.3.3 Three Flexibility Mechanisms

| Mechanism                                | Meaning                                                                                                                                                             |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Clean Development Mechanism (CDM)     | Allowed a developed country with a Kyoto commitment to earn certified emission reductions (CERs) from eligible emission-reduction projects in developing countries. |
| 2. Joint Implementation (JI)             | Allowed a country with a Kyoto commitment to earn emission reduction units (ERUs) from eligible projects in another country with a Kyoto commitment.                |
| 3. International Emissions Trading (IET) | Allowed countries with Kyoto commitments to trade eligible emission units under the Protocol's rules.                                                               |

Prelims distinction: CDM involved projects in developing countries, whereas JI involved projects between countries with Kyoto commitments.

## 11.4 Paris Agreement

### 11.4.1 Establishment and Temperature Goals

- Adopted: 12 December 2015, at COP21 in Paris, France.
- Entered into force: 4 November 2016.
- Parent convention: UNFCCC.
- Coverage: Applies to developed and developing country Parties.

Temperature goals:

1. Hold the increase in global average temperature well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels.
2. Pursue efforts to limit the temperature increase to 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels.

### 11.4.2 Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs)

NDCs are the climate commitments prepared and communicated by individual countries under the Paris Agreement.

- Countries determine their own national climate contributions.
- Parties must prepare, communicate and maintain successive NDCs.
- Successive NDCs are communicated every five years.
- Each successive NDC is expected to represent progression and reflect the country's highest possible ambition.
- Countries must pursue domestic mitigation measures with the aim of achieving their NDC objectives.

Important distinction: The Paris Agreement creates binding procedural obligations concerning NDCs, but achieving every stated NDC emission target is not itself a legally binding result obligation.

### 11.4.3 Global Stocktake (GST)

The Global Stocktake is a periodic assessment of collective progress towards the Paris Agreement's long-term goals.

- Conducted every five years.
- Assesses collective progress in mitigation, adaptation and means of implementation and support.
- First Global Stocktake: Concluded at COP28, Dubai, UAE, in 2023.
- Its outcomes inform countries when updating and strengthening their climate actions and support.

### 11.4.4 Kyoto Protocol vs Paris Agreement

| Feature              | Kyoto Protocol                                               | Paris Agreement                                            |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Adopted              | 1997                                                         | 2015                                                       |
| Main approach        | Binding quantified targets for specified developed countries | Nationally determined climate commitments from all Parties |
| Developing countries | No binding quantified emission-reduction targets             | Must prepare and communicate NDCs                          |
| Climate commitments  | Assigned targets under the Protocol                          | Successive NDCs, communicated every five years             |
| Temperature goal     | No explicit 1.5°C or 2°C treaty target                       | Well below 2°C; pursue 1.5°C                               |

## 11.5 Montreal Protocol and Kigali Amendment

### 11.5.1 Montreal Protocol

- Full name: Montreal Protocol on Substances that Deplete the Ozone Layer.
- Adopted: 1987.
- Entered into force: 1989.
- Parent convention: Vienna Convention for the Protection of the Ozone Layer (1985).
- Objective: Phase out the production and consumption of specified ozone-depleting substances.

Examples of controlled substances include chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) and halons.

### 11.5.2 Kigali Amendment

- Adopted: 2016, Kigali, Rwanda.
- Entered into force: 1 January 2019.
- Amends: Montreal Protocol.
- Objective: Gradually reduce the production and consumption of hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs).

Why HFCs are important:

- HFCs are widely used in refrigeration and air-conditioning.
- HFCs generally do not deplete the ozone layer.
- Many HFCs are powerful greenhouse gases with high global warming potential.

Prelims distinction: The Montreal Protocol originally focused on ozone-depleting substances. The Kigali Amendment addresses HFCs primarily because of their contribution to global warming.

# 12. Important International Climate Organisations

## 12.1 Major Organisations and Their Roles

| Organisation                                     | Establishment                | Main role                                                                                                         |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| IPCC — Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change | 1988                         | Assesses scientific knowledge concerning climate change.                                                          |
| WMO — World Meteorological Organization          | 1950                         | Coordinates international cooperation in meteorology, climate and related observations.                           |
| UNEP — United Nations Environment Programme      | 1972                         | Coordinates UN environmental activities and supports international environmental cooperation.                     |
| UNFCCC Secretariat                               | Established under the UNFCCC | Supports implementation of the Convention, Kyoto Protocol and Paris Agreement and organises climate negotiations. |

## 12.2 Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC)

- Established: 1988 by WMO and UNEP.
- Nature: Intergovernmental scientific assessment body.
- Main function: Assesses published scientific, technical and socioeconomic literature relating to climate change.
- Reports: Assessment Reports and Special Reports.

### Three IPCC Working Groups

| Working Group     | Subject                                  |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Working Group I   | Physical Science Basis of Climate Change |
| Working Group II  | Impacts, Adaptation and Vulnerability    |
| Working Group III | Mitigation of Climate Change             |

Important facts:

- IPCC does not conduct original scientific research.
- It assesses existing research to inform policymakers.
- IPCC reports are policy-relevant but not policy-prescriptive.
- IPCC is not the same as the UNFCCC: IPCC assesses scientific evidence; UNFCCC provides the treaty framework for climate negotiations.

# 13. India and Climate Change

## 13.1 National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC)

### 13.1.1 Establishment and Objective

- Launched: 30 June 2008.
- Government: Government of India.
- Objective: Address climate change through mitigation and adaptation while pursuing sustainable development.
- Original structure: Eight national missions.

### 13.1.2 Eight Original National Missions

| No. | National Mission                                           | Main focus                                                                  |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | National Solar Mission                                     | Promote solar energy generation and utilisation.                            |
| 2   | National Mission for Enhanced Energy Efficiency            | Improve energy efficiency across sectors.                                   |
| 3   | National Mission on Sustainable Habitat                    | Promote energy efficiency, sustainable urban planning and waste management. |
| 4   | National Water Mission                                     | Improve water conservation, efficiency and management.                      |
| 5   | National Mission for Sustaining the Himalayan Ecosystem    | Protect Himalayan ecosystems and improve understanding of climate impacts.  |
| 6   | National Mission for a Green India                         | Increase forest and tree cover and improve ecosystem services.              |
| 7   | National Mission for Sustainable Agriculture               | Improve agricultural resilience to climate change.                          |
| 8   | National Mission on Strategic Knowledge for Climate Change | Strengthen climate research, knowledge and scientific capacity.             |

Important update: The original NAPCC had eight missions. The government's current NAPCC dashboard refers to nine national missions, reflecting the subsequent addition of the National Mission on Sustainable Agriculture's related health-focused climate initiative as a separate National Mission on Health and Climate Change. The original eight-mission list remains important for historical Prelims questions.&#x20;

## 13.2 India's Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs)

India submits its climate commitments under the Paris Agreement.

### 13.2.1 India's Updated NDC (2022): Targets for 2030

India updated its first NDC in August 2022.

| Commitment                      | Target for 2030                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Emissions intensity of GDP      | Reduce by 45% compared with the 2005 level.                                                                               |
| Non-fossil electricity capacity | Achieve approximately 50% of cumulative installed electric power capacity from non-fossil-fuel sources.                   |
| Additional carbon sink          | Create an additional 2.5–3 billion tonnes of CO₂ equivalent through additional forest and tree cover, compared with 2005. |

The updated NDC also includes sustainable lifestyles through LiFE (Lifestyle for Environment), adaptation, climate finance and capacity-building commitments.&#x20;

## 13.4 Important Concepts for UPSC Prelims

| Term                       | Correct meaning                                                                                            |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Emissions intensity of GDP | Greenhouse gas emissions per unit of GDP.                                                                  |
| Absolute emissions         | Total quantity of greenhouse gases emitted.                                                                |
| Installed power capacity   | Maximum rated electricity-generating capacity of installed power plants.                                   |
| Electricity generation     | Actual electricity produced over a period.                                                                 |
| Non-fossil energy          | Includes renewable energy and nuclear energy.                                                              |
| Carbon sink target         | Additional carbon stored or removed through specified activities, such as increased forest and tree cover. |

Important distinctions:

- A reduction in emissions intensity does not necessarily mean a reduction in absolute emissions.
- A 60% non-fossil share of installed electricity capacity does not mean that 60% of electricity actually generated comes from non-fossil sources.
- Non-fossil energy and renewable energy are not identical because nuclear energy is non-fossil but not renewable.
