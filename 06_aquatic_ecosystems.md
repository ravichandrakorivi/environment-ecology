# Chapter 6: Aquatic Ecosystems and Wetlands
## UPSC Civil Services Preliminary Examination – Study Notes

# 6.1 Aquatic Ecosystems: Basic Concepts

An **aquatic ecosystem** is an ecosystem in which organisms interact with one another and with the physical and chemical environment of a water body.

### Classification of Aquatic Ecosystems

| Type | Subtype | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Freshwater | Lentic (standing water) | Lakes, ponds |
| Freshwater | Lotic (flowing water) | Rivers, streams |
| Freshwater | Wetlands | Marshes, swamps |
| Brackish water | Transitional ecosystems | Estuaries, coastal lagoons |
| Marine | Coastal ecosystems | Mangroves, coral reefs, seagrass meadows |
| Marine | Open-ocean ecosystems | Pelagic and deep-sea ecosystems |

### Important Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Lentic | Standing or relatively still water |
| Lotic | Flowing water |
| Freshwater | Water with low dissolved salt concentration |
| Brackish water | Water with salinity between freshwater and seawater |
| Salinity | Concentration of dissolved salts in water |
| Plankton | Organisms drifting with water currents |
| Nekton | Actively swimming aquatic organisms |
| Benthos | Organisms living on or in the bottom of water bodies |
| Periphyton | Organisms attached to submerged surfaces |
| Neuston | Organisms living at the air–water interface |

**Prelims Trap:** Aquatic organisms are not classified only as plankton and nekton. Benthos, periphyton and neuston are also important ecological groups.

---

# 6.2 Classification of Aquatic Organisms

| Category | Characteristics | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Phytoplankton | Photosynthetic plankton | Diatoms, dinoflagellates |
| Zooplankton | Heterotrophic plankton | Copepods, rotifers |
| Nekton | Actively swimming organisms | Fish, squid, whales |
| Benthos | Bottom-dwelling organisms | Clams, sea cucumbers |
| Periphyton | Attached aquatic organisms | Algae on submerged rocks |
| Neuston | Organisms associated with water surface | Water striders |

### Important Facts

- Phytoplankton are major primary producers in aquatic ecosystems.
- Zooplankton commonly feed on phytoplankton and other microorganisms.
- Whales and dolphins are classified as **nekton** based on their movement, although they are mammals.
- Benthos includes both plants and animals, as well as microorganisms.

---

# 6.3 Zones of Aquatic Ecosystems

## A. Lake Zones

| Zone | Characteristics |
|---|---|
| Littoral | Shallow nearshore zone; sunlight generally reaches the bottom |
| Limnetic | Open, sunlit water away from the shore |
| Profundal | Deeper water receiving insufficient light for substantial photosynthesis |
| Benthic | Bottom sediments and associated organisms |

### Important Facts

- Rooted aquatic plants commonly occur in the littoral zone.
- Phytoplankton are important producers in the limnetic zone.
- The profundal zone may experience low dissolved oxygen.
- The benthic zone occurs at the bottom and can overlap spatially with other zones.

## B. Marine Zones

| Zone | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Intertidal | Area between high- and low-tide levels |
| Neritic | Water over the continental shelf |
| Oceanic | Open ocean beyond the continental shelf |
| Pelagic | Open-water environment |
| Benthic | Seafloor environment |
| Photic | Zone receiving sufficient sunlight for photosynthesis |
| Aphotic | Zone without sufficient sunlight for photosynthesis |

**Important:** The photic zone is not defined by a fixed depth everywhere. Its depth varies with water clarity and light penetration.

# 6.4 Lake Stratification

Lakes may develop distinct layers because of differences in water temperature and density.

### Thermal Layers

| Layer | Characteristics |
|---|---|
| Epilimnion | Upper, relatively warm and well-mixed layer |
| Metalimnion | Transition layer containing the thermocline |
| Hypolimnion | Lower, relatively cold and dense layer |

### Thermocline

A layer in which water temperature changes rapidly with depth.

### Important Facts

- Thermal stratification can restrict mixing between surface and deep water.
- Deep-water oxygen depletion may occur when decomposition consumes oxygen faster than it is replenished.
- Seasonal mixing can redistribute oxygen and nutrients.
- Not all lakes develop strong or persistent thermal stratification.

# 6.5 Wetlands

## Definition

Under the **Ramsar Convention**, wetlands include areas of marsh, fen, peatland or water, whether natural or artificial, permanent or temporary, with static or flowing water, fresh, brackish or salt.

They also include areas of marine water whose depth at low tide does not exceed **6 metres**.

### Important Features

- Wetlands may be natural or artificial.
- They may contain freshwater, brackish water or saline water.
- They may be permanent or seasonal.
- They may be inland or coastal.

**Prelims Trap:** Wetlands are not restricted to freshwater lakes and marshes.

---

# 6.6 Classification of Wetlands

## A. Inland Wetlands

| Type | Characteristics | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Lakes | Standing water bodies | Loktak Lake |
| Marshes | Dominated by herbaceous wetland vegetation | Freshwater marshes |
| Swamps | Often dominated by trees or shrubs | Forested swamps |
| Peatlands | Accumulate partially decomposed organic matter | Bogs and fens |
| Floodplains | Periodically inundated land adjoining rivers | Brahmaputra floodplains |
| Oxbow lakes | Former river meanders cut off from the main channel | Floodplain oxbow lakes |

## B. Coastal Wetlands

| Type | Characteristics | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Estuaries | River water mixes with seawater | Hooghly estuary |
| Coastal lagoons | Shallow coastal water bodies separated partly from the sea | Chilika Lake |
| Mangrove wetlands | Intertidal woody vegetation | Sundarbans |
| Salt marshes | Salt-tolerant herbaceous vegetation | Coastal salt marshes |
| Tidal flats | Intertidal mud or sand surfaces | Gulf of Kachchh |
| Seagrass beds | Submerged marine flowering plants | Gulf of Mannar |

### Marsh vs Swamp

| Marsh | Swamp |
|---|---|
| Dominated by grasses, sedges and other herbaceous plants | Often dominated by trees and shrubs |
| Common in shallow wetlands | Common in forested wetlands |
| May be freshwater or saline | May be freshwater or saline |

# 6.7 Ecological Importance of Wetlands

Wetlands provide several ecosystem services.

| Service | Ecological Function |
|---|---|
| Flood regulation | Store and slow floodwater |
| Water purification | Trap sediments and transform or retain some pollutants |
| Groundwater interactions | May recharge or discharge groundwater depending on local conditions |
| Carbon storage | Store carbon in vegetation and sediments |
| Biodiversity conservation | Provide habitats for fish, birds, amphibians and other organisms |
| Shoreline protection | Reduce erosion and wave impacts in suitable coastal settings |
| Nutrient cycling | Support microbial and biological nutrient transformations |
| Livelihood support | Fisheries, agriculture and other resources |

### Important Facts

- Wetlands are sometimes described as the **kidneys of the landscape** because of their role in water purification.
- Wetlands may act as carbon sinks, but some also emit methane.
- Their net climate impact depends on wetland type and environmental conditions.
- Wetlands are not necessarily wastelands or unproductive land.

# 6.8 Ramsar Convention

### Basic Facts

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Full name | Convention on Wetlands of International Importance Especially as Waterfowl Habitat |
| Adopted | **2 February 1971** |
| Place | Ramsar, Iran |
| Entered into force | **1975** |
| India became a Contracting Party | **1982** |
| World Wetlands Day | **2 February** |
| Principal objective | Conservation and wise use of wetlands |

### Three Pillars of the Ramsar Convention

1. Wise use of all wetlands.
2. Designation and effective management of Wetlands of International Importance.
3. International cooperation concerning shared wetlands and species.

### Important Distinctions

- Ramsar designation does **not automatically make a wetland a national park**.
- A Ramsar site may overlap with a wildlife sanctuary, national park or other protected area.
- Ramsar sites can include natural and human-made wetlands.
- The Ramsar Convention is an intergovernmental treaty, not a UNESCO World Heritage Convention.

---

# 6.9 Ramsar Site Selection Criteria

A wetland may qualify under one or more of **nine criteria**.

### Important Criteria for UPSC

| Criterion | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Criterion 1 | Representative, rare or unique wetland type |
| Criterion 2 | Supports vulnerable, endangered or critically endangered species or threatened ecological communities |
| Criterion 3 | Supports populations important for maintaining regional biological diversity |
| Criterion 4 | Supports species at critical life stages or provides refuge during adverse conditions |
| Criterion 5 | Regularly supports **20,000 or more waterbirds** |
| Criterion 6 | Regularly supports **1% of individuals in a population of a waterbird species or subspecies** |
| Criterion 7 | Supports significant indigenous fish biodiversity |
| Criterion 8 | Important fish spawning, nursery, feeding or migration habitat |
| Criterion 9 | Regularly supports 1% of individuals in a population of a wetland-dependent non-avian animal species or subspecies |

### Important Distinction

- **20,000 waterbirds:** Criterion 5.
- **1% of a waterbird population:** Criterion 6.
- **1% of a wetland-dependent non-avian animal population:** Criterion 9.

# 6.13 Estuaries

An **estuary** is a partially enclosed coastal water body where freshwater from rivers mixes with seawater.

### Important Characteristics

- Variable salinity.
- Nutrient-rich conditions in many estuaries.
- Important nursery habitats for fish and crustaceans.
- Strong influence of tides and river discharge.

### Important Indian Estuaries

- Hooghly estuary.
- Mahanadi estuary.
- Godavari estuary.
- Krishna estuary.
- Narmada estuary.
- Tapi estuary.

### Estuary vs Delta

| Estuary | Delta |
|---|---|
| Coastal water body where freshwater and seawater mix | Depositional landform formed by river sediments |
| Influenced by tides and river flow | Influenced by sediment supply, waves, tides and currents |
| Example: Narmada estuary | Example: Ganga–Brahmaputra delta |

**Important:** Estuaries and deltas are not mutually exclusive. Deltaic regions can contain estuaries and tidal channels.

# 6.14 Mangrove Ecosystems

Mangroves are coastal ecosystems dominated by salt-tolerant woody plants adapted to intertidal environments.

### Important Characteristics

- Occur mainly along tropical and subtropical coasts.
- Tolerate saline and waterlogged conditions.
- Support fish, crustaceans, birds and other wildlife.
- Provide coastal protection and carbon storage.

## Mangrove Adaptations

| Adaptation | Function |
|---|---|
| Pneumatophores | Facilitate gas exchange in waterlogged soils |
| Prop roots | Provide support and stability |
| Salt exclusion | Restricts salt entry |
| Salt excretion | Removes excess salt |
| Vivipary | Seeds germinate while attached to the parent plant in certain species |

### Important Mangrove Genera

| Genus | Important Feature |
|---|---|
| *Rhizophora* | Prop roots |
| *Avicennia* | Pneumatophores |
| *Sonneratia* | Pneumatophores |
| *Bruguiera* | Knee roots in some species |
| *Heritiera* | Includes sundari |

### Important Mangrove Regions in India

- Sundarbans, West Bengal.
- Bhitarkanika, Odisha.
- Godavari–Krishna delta.
- Pichavaram, Tamil Nadu.
- Gulf of Kachchh, Gujarat.
- Andaman and Nicobar Islands.

### Important Facts

- Mangroves are **flowering plants**, not algae.
- They are adapted to saline conditions but do not all require high salinity for growth.
- Mangroves can occur in freshwater-influenced coastal environments.
- Mangrove ecosystems are important **blue carbon** habitats.

# 6.15 Coral Reef Ecosystems

## Corals

Corals are marine invertebrate animals belonging to phylum **Cnidaria**.

Reef-building corals commonly live in association with photosynthetic dinoflagellates.

### Important Facts

- Corals are animals, not plants.
- Many reef-building corals secrete calcium carbonate skeletons.
- Coral reefs support high marine biodiversity.
- Reef-building corals generally require suitable light, temperature, salinity and water clarity.

## Types of Coral Reefs

| Type | Characteristics |
|---|---|
| Fringing reef | Develops close to the shoreline |
| Barrier reef | Separated from the shore by a lagoon |
| Atoll | Ring-shaped reef enclosing a lagoon |

### Important Indian Coral Reef Regions

| Gulf of Kachchh | Coral reefs in the northwestern Indian coast |
| Gulf of Mannar | Marine biodiversity and coral reefs |

# 6.16 Coral Bleaching

Coral bleaching occurs when corals lose their symbiotic algae or the algae's photosynthetic pigments, causing the coral tissue to become pale or transparent.

### Major Causes

- Elevated sea surface temperatures.
- Marine heatwaves.
- Extreme light stress.
- Certain pollution and environmental stressors.

### Important Facts

- Bleaching does **not necessarily mean immediate coral death**.
- Corals may recover if environmental conditions improve.
- Prolonged or severe bleaching can cause coral mortality.
- Mass bleaching events are strongly associated with marine heat stress.

### Coral Bleaching vs Ocean Acidification

| Coral Bleaching | Ocean Acidification |
|---|---|
| Often triggered by elevated temperature | Caused primarily by ocean absorption of atmospheric CO₂ |
| Involves loss of symbiotic algae or pigments | Reduces seawater pH and carbonate-ion availability |
| Can impair coral nutrition and survival | Can impair calcification in many marine organisms |

# 6.17 Seagrass Ecosystems

Seagrasses are **marine flowering plants** adapted to living submerged in saline water.

### Important Features

- Possess roots, stems, leaves, flowers and seeds.
- Form underwater meadows.
- Provide habitats for fish and invertebrates.
- Store carbon in vegetation and sediments.
- Help stabilize sediments.

### Important Indian Seagrass Regions

- Gulf of Mannar.
- Palk Bay.
- Gulf of Kachchh.
- Andaman and Nicobar Islands.
- Lakshadweep.

### Important Fauna

**Dugong**
- Marine herbivorous mammal.
- Feeds primarily on seagrasses.
- IUCN status: Vulnerable.

### Seagrass vs Seaweed

| Seagrass | Seaweed |
|---|---|
| Flowering plant | Alga |
| Has true roots, stems and leaves | Does not possess true roots, stems and leaves |
| Produces flowers and seeds | Reproduces through algal reproductive structures |
| Example: *Halophila* | Example: *Sargassum* |

**UPSC Trap:** Seagrasses are not marine algae.

# 6.18 Eutrophication

**Eutrophication** is the enrichment of a water body with nutrients, particularly nitrogen and phosphorus, leading to increased biological productivity and often undesirable ecological changes.

### Causes

- Agricultural fertilizer runoff.
- Untreated sewage.
- Animal waste.
- Industrial effluents containing nutrients.

### Process

1. Excess nutrients enter a water body.
2. Algal and cyanobacterial growth may increase.
3. Excess biomass dies and decomposes.
4. Microbial decomposition consumes dissolved oxygen.
5. Hypoxia or anoxia may develop.
6. Fish and other aquatic organisms may die or migrate.

### Important Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Algal bloom | Rapid increase in algal abundance |
| Harmful algal bloom | Bloom causing ecological or health-related harm |
| Hypoxia | Low dissolved oxygen |
| Anoxia | Absence of dissolved oxygen |
| Dead zone | Area where oxygen depletion severely restricts many aquatic organisms |

### Important Facts

- Phosphorus is often a limiting nutrient in freshwater ecosystems.
- Nitrogen frequently plays an important limiting role in marine ecosystems.
- Nutrient limitation varies with location and environmental conditions.
- Not all algal blooms are toxic.

# 6.19 Biological Oxygen Demand (BOD) and Dissolved Oxygen (DO)

## Biological Oxygen Demand

BOD measures the amount of dissolved oxygen consumed by microorganisms while decomposing biodegradable organic matter under specified test conditions.

### Important Facts

- High BOD generally indicates substantial biodegradable organic pollution.
- BOD is commonly measured over five days at a specified temperature.
- Higher BOD can contribute to lower dissolved oxygen.

## Dissolved Oxygen

DO is the concentration of oxygen dissolved in water.

### Important Facts

- Aquatic organisms depend on dissolved oxygen.
- Oxygen enters water through atmospheric exchange and photosynthesis.
- Respiration and decomposition consume oxygen.
- Warm water generally holds less dissolved oxygen at saturation than cold water.

### BOD vs DO

| BOD | DO |
|---|---|
| Oxygen demand associated with biodegradation | Oxygen present in water |
| Higher values often indicate organic pollution | Lower values may indicate oxygen stress |
| Measures oxygen consumption under test conditions | Measures available dissolved oxygen |

**Prelims Trap:** High BOD is generally undesirable for water quality.

# 6.20 Ocean Productivity and Upwelling

## Upwelling

Upwelling is the movement of deeper, generally colder and nutrient-rich water towards the ocean surface.

### Importance

- Brings nutrients into the sunlit zone.
- Supports phytoplankton growth.
- Enhances marine food-web productivity.
- Supports productive fishing grounds.

### Important Upwelling Regions

- Peru–Chile coast.
- California coast.
- Northwest African coast.
- Southwest African coast.

### UPSC PYQ – 2011

**Question:** What is the ecological importance of upwelling zones?

**Answer:** They bring nutrient-rich water towards the surface, increasing primary productivity.

# 6.21 Blue Carbon Ecosystems

**Blue carbon** refers to carbon captured and stored by coastal and marine ecosystems.

### Important Blue Carbon Ecosystems

1. Mangrove forests.
2. Seagrass meadows.
3. Tidal salt marshes.

### Important Facts

- Blue carbon ecosystems can store substantial carbon in sediments.
- They also provide coastal protection and biodiversity benefits.
- Degradation may release previously stored carbon.
- Coral reefs are not generally included among the three principal vegetated coastal blue carbon ecosystems.

# 6.22 Wetland Conservation in India

## A. Wetlands (Conservation and Management) Rules, 2017

Notified under the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**.

### Important Features

- Provide a regulatory framework for conservation and management of notified wetlands.
- Establish State/UT Wetland Authorities.
- Prohibit specified activities in notified wetlands.
- Require preparation of inventories and management-related documentation.

### Important Prohibited Activities

Subject to the scope and provisions of the Rules:

- Conversion of wetlands for non-wetland uses.
- Setting up or expansion of industries.
- Disposal of solid waste.
- Discharge of untreated wastes and effluents.
- Certain permanent construction activities.

### Important Legal Distinction

The 2017 Rules exclude certain categories of wetlands from their direct scope, including specified wetlands regulated under other legal frameworks.

**Prelims Trap:** It is incorrect to assume that every water body in India is regulated identically under the Wetlands Rules, 2017.

---

## B. National Plan for Conservation of Aquatic Ecosystems (NPCA)

### Important Facts

- Centrally sponsored scheme.
- Integrates earlier wetland and lake conservation programmes.
- Supports conservation and management of identified wetlands and lakes.
- Implemented through coordination between the Central and State Governments.

### Objectives

- Improve water quality.
- Restore aquatic habitats.
- Control pollution.
- Conserve biodiversity.
- Promote sustainable management.

---

## C. Amrit Dharohar

- Announced in the **Union Budget 2023–24**.
- Focuses on conservation and sustainable use of Ramsar sites.
- Promotes biodiversity, carbon storage, ecotourism and livelihood opportunities.
