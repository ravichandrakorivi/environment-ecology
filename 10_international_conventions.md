# 10.2 Ramsar Convention, 1971

### Important Facts

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Adopted | **2 February 1971** |
| Place | Ramsar, Iran |
| Entered into force | 1975 |
| India became a party | 1982 |
| World Wetlands Day | 2 February |
| Secretariat | Gland, Switzerland |

### Objective

Conservation and **wise use of wetlands** through national action and international cooperation.

### Three Pillars

1. Wise use of all wetlands.
2. Designation and management of Ramsar sites.
3. International cooperation concerning shared wetlands and species.

### Important Facts

- Ramsar sites are wetlands of international importance.
- Ramsar designation does not automatically create a national park or wildlife sanctuary.
- Wetlands may be natural or artificial.
- The Ramsar definition includes specified marine waters not exceeding **6 metres in depth at low tide**.

### Montreux Record

A register of Ramsar sites where adverse changes in ecological character have occurred, are occurring or are likely to occur.

**Indian sites on the Montreux Record:**
- Keoladeo National Park, Rajasthan.
- Loktak Lake, Manipur.

**Chilika Lake**, Odisha, was removed from the Montreux Record in **2002**.

# 10.3 Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species (CITES)

### Important Facts

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Full name | Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora |
| Adopted | **1973** |
| Entered into force | **1975** |
| Secretariat | Geneva, Switzerland |
| Administered by | UNEP |
| India became a party | **1976** |

### Objective

Ensure that international trade in specimens of wild animals and plants does not threaten their survival.

## CITES Appendices

| Appendix | Meaning | Trade Regulation |
|---|---|---|
| **Appendix I** | Species threatened with extinction that are or may be affected by trade | International commercial trade in wild-sourced specimens is generally prohibited |
| **Appendix II** | Species not necessarily threatened with extinction but whose trade must be controlled | International trade permitted subject to applicable controls |
| **Appendix III** | Species protected in at least one country that seeks cooperation from other countries in controlling trade | Trade regulated through specified documentation |

### Important Facts

- CITES regulates **international trade**, not every form of domestic wildlife trade.
- Appendix I does not mean that all trade under every circumstance is absolutely prohibited.
- Appendix II species are not necessarily endangered.
- Appendix III listing may be initiated by an individual party.

### CITES vs IUCN Red List

| CITES | IUCN Red List |
|---|---|
| International treaty | Scientific conservation-status assessment system |
| Regulates international trade | Assesses extinction risk |
| Uses Appendices I, II and III | Uses categories such as CR, EN and VU |
| Legally binding on parties | Red List categories are not themselves legally binding |

**UPSC Trap:** A species listed as Endangered by IUCN is not automatically included in CITES Appendix I.

# 10.5 Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD)

### Important Facts

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Adopted | **1992** |
| Opened for signature | Rio Earth Summit, 1992 |
| Entered into force | **1993** |
| Secretariat | Montreal, Canada |

### Three Objectives

1. Conservation of biological diversity.

- Access to genetic resources is subject to applicable legal requirements.

# 10.6 Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety

### Important Facts

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Adopted | **2000** |
| Entered into force | **2003** |
| Parent convention | CBD |
| Main subject | Living modified organisms |

### Objective

Ensure adequate protection in the safe transfer, handling and use of **living modified organisms (LMOs)** resulting from modern biotechnology, particularly those that may adversely affect biodiversity.

### Important Features

- Advance Informed Agreement procedure for specified transboundary movements.
- Risk assessment.
- Biosafety Clearing-House.
- Precautionary approach.

### Important Distinction

**Living Modified Organism (LMO)** refers to a living organism possessing a novel combination of genetic material obtained through modern biotechnology.

Not every product derived from genetically modified organisms is itself an LMO.

### Nagoya–Kuala Lumpur Supplementary Protocol

- Adopted in **2010**.
- Addresses liability and redress concerning damage resulting from LMOs.
- Entered into force in **2018**.

# 10.7 Nagoya Protocol, 2010

### Important Facts

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Adopted | **2010** |
| Entered into force | **2014** |
| Parent convention | CBD |
| Main subject | Access and Benefit-Sharing (ABS) |

### Objective

Fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising from the utilization of genetic resources.

### Important Concepts

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Access and Benefit-Sharing | Sharing benefits from the use of genetic resources |
| Prior Informed Consent (PIC) | Consent obtained before access where required |
| Mutually Agreed Terms (MAT) | Agreed conditions governing access and benefit-sharing |
| Traditional Knowledge | Knowledge held by indigenous peoples and local communities |

### Important Facts

- The Nagoya Protocol is concerned with **genetic resources and associated traditional knowledge**.
- It is not principally a treaty on genetically modified organism safety.
- India's Biological Diversity Act provides a domestic framework relevant to access and benefit-sharing.

### Cartagena vs Nagoya

| Cartagena Protocol | Nagoya Protocol |
|---|---|
| Adopted in 2000 | Adopted in 2010 |
| Biosafety | Access and benefit-sharing |
| Living modified organisms | Genetic resources and associated traditional knowledge |
| Risk management | Fair and equitable sharing of benefits |

# 10.8 Kunming–Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework (GBF)

### Important Facts

- Adopted at **CBD COP15 in Montreal, Canada, in December 2022**.
- Contains **4 long-term goals for 2050**.
- Contains **23 action-oriented targets for 2030**.

## Important Targets

| Target | Important Provision |
|---|---|
| **Target 2** | Effective restoration of at least **30% of degraded terrestrial, inland water, coastal and marine ecosystems** by 2030 |
| **Target 3** | Effective conservation and management of at least **30% of terrestrial, inland water, coastal and marine areas** by 2030 |
| **Target 6** | Reduce introduction and establishment of invasive alien species by at least **50%** by 2030 |
| **Target 7** | Reduce pollution risks and negative impacts to levels not harmful to biodiversity |
| **Target 8** | Minimize climate-change impacts on biodiversity and increase resilience |
| **Target 10** | Sustainable management of agriculture, aquaculture, fisheries and forestry |
| **Target 15** | Encourage and enable businesses to assess and disclose biodiversity-related risks, dependencies and impacts |
| **Target 19** | Mobilize at least **USD 200 billion annually by 2030** for biodiversity |

### Target 3: 30×30

The **30×30 target** aims to conserve and effectively manage at least 30% of the world's land, inland waters, coastal areas and oceans by 2030.

**Important:** It is a **global target**, not a requirement that every country individually designate exactly 30% of its territory as national parks.

### Important Distinction

- **30% restoration:** Target 2.
- **30% conservation:** Target 3.

# 10.11 Paris Agreement, 2015

### Important Facts

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Adopted | **2015** |
| COP | COP21 |
| Entered into force | **2016** |
| Parent convention | UNFCCC |

### Temperature Goal

Hold global warming to **well below 2°C** above pre-industrial levels and pursue efforts to limit warming to **1.5°C**.

### Important Features

- Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs).
- Global Stocktake.
- Enhanced Transparency Framework.
- Climate finance.
- Adaptation.
- Article 6 cooperation.

### Important Facts

- Countries communicate successive NDCs on a five-year cycle.
- The first Global Stocktake concluded at **COP28 in 2023**.
- Article 6 includes market and non-market approaches.

### Kyoto vs Paris

| Kyoto Protocol | Paris Agreement |
|---|---|
| Adopted in 1997 | Adopted in 2015 |
| Quantified commitments for specified developed-country parties | NDC framework applicable to all parties |
| CDM, JI and emissions trading | Article 6 cooperative approaches |
| First commitment period began in 2008 | Entered into force in 2016 |

# 10.12 United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD)

### Important Facts

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Adopted | **17 June 1994** |
| Place | Paris, France |
| Entered into force | **26 December 1996** |
| Secretariat | Bonn, Germany |
| Principal focus | Desertification, land degradation and drought |

### Important Concepts

**Desertification:** Land degradation in arid, semi-arid and dry sub-humid areas resulting from various factors, including climatic variations and human activities.

**Land Degradation Neutrality (LDN):** A condition in which the amount and quality of land resources necessary to support ecosystem functions and services remain stable or increase within specified temporal and spatial scales.

### Important Facts

- UNCCD is one of the three Rio Conventions.
- It addresses land degradation and drought, not merely the expansion of sandy deserts.
- Its strategic framework for **2018–2030** supports progress towards land degradation neutrality.
- **SDG Target 15.3** concerns combating desertification and achieving a land degradation-neutral world by 2030. [UNCCD](https://www.unccd.int/convention/ratification?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### World Day to Combat Desertification and Drought

**17 June**

### India and UNCCD

- India hosted **UNCCD COP14** in New Delhi in **2019**.
- India has announced a target to restore **26 million hectares of degraded land by 2030**.

# 10.13 The Three Rio Conventions

| Convention | Adopted | Principal Subject | Secretariat |
|---|---|---|---|
| UNFCCC | 1992 | Climate change | Bonn |
| CBD | 1992 | Biodiversity | Montreal |
| UNCCD | 1994 | Desertification and land degradation | Bonn |

### Important Facts

# 10.14 Basel Convention, 1989

### Important Facts

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Adopted | **1989** |
| Entered into force | **1992** |
| Secretariat | Geneva, Switzerland |
| Principal subject | Transboundary movement and disposal of hazardous wastes |

### Objectives

- Reduce generation of hazardous waste.
- Ensure environmentally sound waste management.
- Regulate transboundary movement of hazardous and specified other wastes.
- Prevent illegal waste trafficking.

### Important Principle

**Prior Informed Consent (PIC):** Specified transboundary waste movements require notification and consent under the Convention.

### Important Facts

- Basel does not impose an absolute prohibition on every international movement of hazardous waste.
- It establishes procedures and restrictions for specified waste movements.
- The **Basel Ban Amendment** entered into force in **2019**.
- Amendments addressing certain plastic-waste movements became effective in **2021**.

**UPSC Trap:** Basel concerns hazardous and specified other wastes; it is not principally a treaty regulating international trade in endangered species.

# 10.15 Rotterdam Convention, 1998

### Important Facts

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Adopted | **1998** |
| Entered into force | **2004** |
| Secretariat | Joint FAO–UNEP arrangements |
| Principal subject | Certain hazardous chemicals and pesticides in international trade |

### Objective

Promote shared responsibility and cooperative efforts in international trade in certain hazardous chemicals.

### Important Mechanism

**Prior Informed Consent (PIC) Procedure**

Importing countries communicate decisions concerning future imports of chemicals listed under the Convention.

### Important Facts

- Rotterdam covers specified hazardous industrial chemicals and pesticides.
- Its PIC procedure does not automatically ban every listed chemical.
- The Convention facilitates information exchange and informed import decisions.

# 10.16 Stockholm Convention, 2001

### Important Facts

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Adopted | **2001** |
| Entered into force | **2004** |
| Secretariat | Geneva, Switzerland |
| Principal subject | Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs) |

### Persistent Organic Pollutants

POPs generally exhibit one or more of the following important characteristics:

1. Persistence in the environment.
2. Bioaccumulation.
3. Potential for long-range environmental transport.
4. Adverse effects on health or the environment.

### Important Examples

- DDT.
- PCBs.
- Dioxins.
- Furans.
- Certain organochlorine pesticides.

### Stockholm Convention Annexes

| Annex | Principal Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Annex A** | Elimination |
| **Annex B** | Restriction |
| **Annex C** | Reduction of unintentional releases |

### Important Facts

- DDT is listed in **Annex B**, subject to specified restrictions and acceptable purposes, including disease-vector control.
- The Convention addresses intentionally produced and unintentionally produced POPs.
- Dioxins and furans are important examples of unintentionally produced POPs.

# 10.17 Basel, Rotterdam and Stockholm Conventions: Comparison

| Feature | Basel | Rotterdam | Stockholm |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adopted | 1989 | 1998 | 2001 |
| Main subject | Hazardous waste movements | Trade in specified hazardous chemicals | Persistent organic pollutants |
| Important mechanism | Prior informed consent for waste movements | PIC procedure for chemical imports | Elimination, restriction and release reduction |
| Principal concern | Waste management | Chemical trade | Persistent pollution |

**Important:** These are commonly called the **BRS Conventions** and have coordinated secretariat arrangements. They remain three distinct treaties. [The United Nations Office at Geneva](https://www.ungeneva.org/en/about/topics/environment?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

# 10.18 Minamata Convention on Mercury

### Important Facts

|---|---|
| Adopted | **2013** |
| Entered into force | **2017** |
| Principal subject | Mercury pollution |
| Named after | Minamata, Japan |

### Objectives

Protect human health and the environment from anthropogenic emissions and releases of mercury and mercury compounds.

### Important Provisions

# 10.19 Vienna Convention and Montreal Protocol

## Vienna Convention, 1985

- Adopted in **1985**.
- Entered into force in **1988**.
- Provides a framework for international cooperation to protect the ozone layer.

## Montreal Protocol, 1987

- Adopted in **1987**.
- Entered into force in **1989**.
- Controls production and consumption of specified ozone-depleting substances.

### Important Ozone-Depleting Substances

- CFCs.
- Halons.
- Carbon tetrachloride.
- Methyl chloroform.
- Methyl bromide.
- HCFCs.

### Kigali Amendment, 2016

- Adopted in **2016**.
- Entered into force in **2019**.
- Provides for the phasedown of **hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs)**.

### Important Distinction

| Ozone-Depleting Substances | HFCs |
|---|---|
| Generally contain ozone-depleting chlorine or bromine | Generally do not deplete stratospheric ozone |
| Controlled under Montreal Protocol provisions | Phased down under Kigali Amendment |
| Examples: CFCs, HCFCs | Examples: HFC-134a and HFC-32 |

**UPSC Trap:** Kigali targets HFCs because of their climate impact, not because they are major ozone-depleting substances.

# 10.24 International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN)

### Important Facts

- Established in **1948**.
- Headquarters: **Gland, Switzerland**.
- Membership includes governments, government agencies and civil society organizations.
- Maintains the **IUCN Red List of Threatened Species**.

### IUCN Red List Categories

| Category | Abbreviation |
|---|---|
| Extinct | EX |
| Extinct in the Wild | EW |
| Critically Endangered | CR |
| Endangered | EN |
| Vulnerable | VU |
| Near Threatened | NT |
| Least Concern | LC |
| Data Deficient | DD |
| Not Evaluated | NE |

### Threatened Categories

The term **threatened species** includes:

1. Critically Endangered.
2. Endangered.
3. Vulnerable.

**Important:** Near Threatened is not included among the three threatened categories.

### IUCN vs WWF

- IUCN is a membership union involving governments and civil society.
- WWF is an international conservation organization.
- Neither is a UN specialized agency.
