# Chapter 5: Forests and Terrestrial Ecosystems
## UPSC Civil Services Preliminary Examination – Study Notes

# 5.1 Forests: Basic Concepts

A **forest** is a terrestrial ecosystem dominated by trees and associated vegetation, animals, microorganisms and their interactions with the physical environment.

### Important Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Forest | Ecosystem dominated by trees and associated organisms |
| Forest cover | Land classified as forest based on tree canopy cover and minimum area criteria |
| Tree cover | Tree patches outside recorded forest areas that do not qualify as forest cover under ISFR mapping criteria |
| Recorded Forest Area (RFA) | Land recorded as forest in government records |
| Forest canopy | Upper layer formed by tree crowns |
| Canopy density | Proportion of ground area covered by the vertical projection of tree crowns |
| Afforestation | Establishment of forest on land not previously forested, or without forest for a long period |
| Reforestation | Re-establishment of forest on previously forested land |
| Deforestation | Conversion of forest to non-forest land use |
| Forest degradation | Reduction in forest quality, structure, productivity or ecological functions |

**Important:** Forest cover and recorded forest area are different concepts.

- Forest cover is assessed primarily through satellite imagery.
- Recorded forest area is based on government land records.
- Forest cover may include tree plantations and orchards meeting the assessment criteria.
- Recorded forest area may contain land without substantial tree cover.

---

# 5.2 Classification of Forests in India

Indian forests can be classified according to:

1. Climate and vegetation.
2. Canopy density.
3. Legal status.
4. Administrative ownership and management.

## A. Classification Based on Climate and Vegetation

### Major Forest Types

| Forest Type | Climatic Conditions | Important Vegetation | Distribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tropical evergreen | High rainfall, warm and humid | Ebony, mahogany, rosewood | Western Ghats, Northeast India, Andaman and Nicobar Islands |
| Tropical semi-evergreen | High rainfall with some seasonal dryness | Evergreen and deciduous species | Western Ghats, Northeast India |
| Tropical moist deciduous | Moderate to high seasonal rainfall | Sal, teak, bamboo | Eastern India, Himalayan foothills, parts of Western Ghats |
| Tropical dry deciduous | Moderate rainfall with prolonged dry season | Teak, tendu, palash | Central and peninsular India |
| Tropical thorn | Low rainfall | Acacia, Prosopis, thorny shrubs | Rajasthan, Gujarat and other semi-arid regions |
| Littoral and swamp | Coastal and waterlogged conditions | Mangroves and swamp vegetation | Coastal India, Sundarbans |
| Subtropical forests | Subtropical hills | Chir pine, broadleaf species | Himalayan foothills and lower slopes |
| Temperate forests | Cool mountainous climate | Oak, deodar, fir, spruce | Himalayas |
| Subalpine forests | Near upper tree line | Birch, fir, rhododendron | Higher Himalayas |
| Alpine vegetation | Above tree line | Alpine grasses, herbs, shrubs | High Himalayas |

**Important:** These are broad ecological groupings. The detailed Champion and Seth classification contains additional forest types and subtypes.

# 5.3 Tropical Evergreen Forests

### Characteristics

- Occur in regions with high rainfall and relatively limited seasonal water stress.
- Trees generally retain foliage throughout the year, although individual leaves are replaced.
- Dense, multilayered vegetation.
- High species richness.
- Many epiphytes, climbers and lianas.

### Distribution in India

- Western Ghats.
- Northeast India.
- Andaman and Nicobar Islands.

### Important Trees

- Ebony.
- Rosewood.
- Various dipterocarps.
- Other evergreen hardwood species.

### Important Facts

- Evergreen forests do not necessarily consist of trees that never shed leaves.
- Leaf shedding occurs at different times rather than simultaneously across the entire forest.
- Dense canopy cover reduces the amount of sunlight reaching the forest floor.

---

# 5.4 Tropical Deciduous Forests

Deciduous forests are characterized by trees that shed leaves during an unfavourable season, commonly the dry season in tropical India.

## Types

### A. Tropical Moist Deciduous Forests

- Occur in regions with relatively high seasonal rainfall.
- Trees shed leaves during the dry season.
- Important species include sal, teak and bamboo.

**Distribution:**
- Eastern India.
- Himalayan foothills.
- Parts of Central India.
- Western Ghats foothills and adjoining areas.

### B. Tropical Dry Deciduous Forests

- Occur in regions with lower rainfall and longer dry periods.
- Canopy is generally more open than in moist deciduous forests.
- Important species include teak, tendu, palash and various acacias.

**Distribution:**
- Central India.
- Deccan Plateau.
- Parts of northern and peninsular India.

### Comparison

| Moist Deciduous | Dry Deciduous |
|---|---|
| Relatively higher rainfall | Relatively lower rainfall |
| Denser canopy | More open canopy |
| Shorter dry season | Longer dry season |
| Sal and teak forests | Teak, tendu and dry forest species |

**UPSC Trap:** Sal and teak are not exclusively restricted to one single forest type.

---

# 5.5 Tropical Thorn Forests and Scrub

### Characteristics

- Occur in arid and semi-arid regions.
- Low and highly seasonal rainfall.
- Vegetation includes thorny trees, shrubs and grasses.
- Plants exhibit adaptations to water scarcity.

### Important Adaptations

1. Reduced leaf area.
2. Thick cuticle.
3. Deep or extensive root systems.
4. Leaf shedding during drought.
5. Water storage in some species.

### Distribution

- Western Rajasthan.
- Gujarat.
- Parts of the Deccan Plateau.
- Other dry regions of India.

### Important Plants

| Plant | Important Association |
|---|---|
| Khejri (*Prosopis cineraria*) | Arid Rajasthan |
| Babul (*Vachellia nilotica*) | Dry and semi-arid habitats |
| Ber (*Ziziphus mauritiana*) | Dry tropical regions |
| Cactus | Xerophytic vegetation; many species are introduced in India |
| *Prosopis juliflora* | Introduced and invasive in many dryland ecosystems |

**Important:** Thorn forests are not ecologically equivalent to deserts devoid of vegetation.

# 5.6 Montane Forests

Montane vegetation changes with altitude, temperature, moisture and slope exposure.

## Broad Altitudinal Sequence in the Himalayas

| Altitudinal Zone | Characteristic Vegetation |
|---|---|
| Lower foothills | Tropical and subtropical forests |
| Lower mountain slopes | Subtropical broadleaf and pine forests |
| Middle elevations | Temperate broadleaf and coniferous forests |
| Higher elevations | Subalpine forests |
| Above tree line | Alpine shrubs, meadows and herbs |

**Important:** These zones overlap, and their actual elevations vary across the Himalayas.

### Important Himalayan Trees

| Species | Important Habitat |
|---|---|
| Chir pine | Subtropical Himalayan slopes |
| Deodar | Western Himalayan temperate forests |
| Blue pine | Himalayan temperate forests |
| Oak | Temperate Himalayan forests |
| Fir | Cool temperate and subalpine forests |
| Spruce | Himalayan temperate forests |
| Birch | Upper forest zones |
| Rhododendron | Temperate and subalpine habitats |

### Himalayan Treeline

The **treeline** is the upper altitudinal or latitudinal limit beyond which trees cannot form established stands under prevailing environmental conditions.

Factors influencing treeline:
- Temperature.
- Growing-season length.
- Wind.
- Snow cover.
- Soil conditions.
- Local topography.

**Prelims Trap:** Treeline is not a fixed elevation throughout the Himalayas.

---

# 5.7 Alpine Ecosystems

Alpine ecosystems occur above the climatic treeline in mountainous regions.

### Characteristics

- Low temperatures.
- Short growing seasons.
- Strong winds.
- Snow cover during substantial parts of the year.
- Vegetation dominated by grasses, sedges, herbs and low shrubs.

### Important Indian Alpine Regions

- Ladakh.
- Himachal Pradesh.
- Uttarakhand.
- Sikkim.
- Arunachal Pradesh.

### Important Fauna

| Species | Habitat |
|---|---|
| Snow leopard | High-altitude mountains |
| Himalayan blue sheep (bharal) | Rocky alpine and subalpine terrain |
| Himalayan ibex | High-altitude mountainous regions |
| Himalayan marmot | Alpine meadows |
| Himalayan tahr | Mountain slopes |
| Kiang | Trans-Himalayan cold deserts |

---

# 5.8 Grassland Ecosystems

Grasslands are terrestrial ecosystems dominated by grasses and other herbaceous vegetation, with relatively sparse tree cover.

### Major Types

| Grassland Type | Characteristics | Indian Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Tropical grasslands | Warm climate, seasonal rainfall | Terai grasslands, savanna-like landscapes |
| Semi-arid grasslands | Low rainfall, drought-adapted vegetation | Rajasthan and Deccan grasslands |
| Alpine grasslands | High-altitude meadows | Himalayan bugyals |
| Floodplain grasslands | Seasonal flooding | Brahmaputra floodplains |

### Important Indian Grasslands

| Grassland | Location | Important Association |
|---|---|---|
| Banni Grasslands | Gujarat | Kachchh grassland ecosystem |
| Terai grasslands | Himalayan foothills | Swamp deer, rhinoceros, grassland birds |
| Shola grasslands | Western Ghats | Montane grassland–forest mosaic |
| Bugyals | Uttarakhand Himalayas | Alpine and subalpine meadows |
| Kaziranga grasslands | Assam | Greater one-horned rhinoceros |
| Velavadar grasslands | Gujarat | Blackbuck |

### Important Facts

- Grasslands are natural ecosystems, not necessarily degraded forests.
- Grassland conservation is essential for species such as the Great Indian bustard and blackbuck.
- Unscientific tree planting in natural grasslands can damage grassland biodiversity.

---

# 5.9 Shola Forests

**Sholas** are patches of montane evergreen forest occurring within grassland landscapes of the higher Western Ghats.

### Distribution

- Nilgiri Hills.
- Anamalai Hills.
- Palani Hills.
- Other high-elevation Western Ghats landscapes.

### Characteristics

- Forest patches commonly occur in sheltered valleys and depressions.
- Grasslands dominate many exposed slopes and hilltops.
- Sholas support distinctive montane biodiversity.
- They contribute to watershed protection and stream regulation.

### Important Fauna

- Nilgiri tahr, primarily associated with adjoining montane grasslands.
- Nilgiri langur.
- Shola-associated endemic birds and amphibians.

**Prelims Trap:** Shola ecosystems are not simply continuous evergreen forests. They form a characteristic **forest–grassland mosaic**.

---

# 5.10 Desert Ecosystems

Deserts are characterized primarily by low water availability rather than necessarily high temperatures.

## Types

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Hot desert | Thar Desert |
| Cold desert | Ladakh and parts of the Trans-Himalaya |

### Hot Desert: Thar

**Important Features**
- Low and variable rainfall.
- High evaporation.
- Sparse xerophytic vegetation.
- Sandy and rocky habitats.

**Important Flora**
- Khejri.
- Ber.
- Acacia and other drought-tolerant shrubs.
- Grasses adapted to arid conditions.

**Important Fauna**
- Great Indian bustard.
- Chinkara.
- Desert fox.
- Desert cat.

### Cold Desert: Ladakh

**Important Features**
- Low precipitation.
- Cold temperatures.
- Sparse vegetation.
- Short growing season.

**Important Fauna**
- Snow leopard.
- Kiang.
- Bharal.
- Himalayan ibex.

### Important Distinction

| Hot Desert | Cold Desert |
|---|---|
| High summer temperatures | Low temperatures for much of the year |
| Water scarcity is the major limitation | Water scarcity and cold temperatures are major limitations |
| Thar Desert | Ladakh |

# 5.11 Forest Canopy Density Classification in India

The **Forest Survey of India (FSI)** classifies forest cover using canopy density.

| Category | Canopy Density |
|---|---|
| Very Dense Forest (VDF) | **70% or more** |
| Moderately Dense Forest (MDF) | **40% to less than 70%** |
| Open Forest (OF) | **10% to less than 40%** |
| Scrub | **Less than 10%** |
| Non-Forest | Land not classified as forest cover |

### Forest Cover Criteria

Under the ISFR methodology, forest cover generally includes land:

- At least **1 hectare** in area.
- With tree canopy density of at least **10%**.
- Irrespective of legal ownership and land use.

**Important:** The FSI definition is an assessment classification and should not be confused with the legal definition of forest.

# 5.13 Forest Carbon Stocks and Carbon Sequestration

Forests influence the carbon cycle through photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition and carbon storage.

### Important Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Carbon stock | Quantity of carbon stored in a reservoir at a particular time |
| Carbon sequestration | Capture and storage of carbon |
| Carbon sink | Reservoir absorbing more carbon than it releases over a specified period |
| Carbon source | Reservoir releasing more carbon than it absorbs |
| Above-ground biomass | Living vegetation above the soil surface |
| Below-ground biomass | Living roots |
| Soil organic carbon | Carbon stored in soil organic matter |

### Major Forest Carbon Pools

1. Above-ground biomass.
2. Below-ground biomass.
3. Deadwood.
4. Litter.
5. Soil organic carbon.

### Important Facts

- Forests store carbon in vegetation and soils.
- Mature forests may continue accumulating carbon, although rates vary.
- Forest degradation can reduce carbon stocks without complete deforestation.
- Forest fires can release stored carbon.
- Soil carbon is an important component of terrestrial carbon storage.

### Carbon Sink vs Carbon Stock

| Carbon Sink | Carbon Stock |
|---|---|
| Refers to net carbon uptake over time | Refers to carbon stored at a given time |
| A rate or net flow concept | A quantity |
| Example: Forest absorbing more CO₂ than it releases | Example: Carbon stored in forest biomass and soil |

# 5.14 Forest Fires

Forest fires are combustion events affecting forest vegetation, litter or soil organic matter.

## Types of Forest Fires

| Type | Characteristics |
|---|---|
| Surface fire | Burns litter, grasses and low vegetation |
| Crown fire | Spreads through tree canopies |
| Ground fire | Burns organic material beneath the surface, including peat |

### Causes

**Natural**
- Lightning.
- Rare natural ignition sources.

**Human-related**
- Agricultural burning.
- Unattended campfires.
- Deliberate ignition.
- Accidental ignition.

### Ecological Effects

- Vegetation damage.
- Wildlife mortality and displacement.
- Carbon emissions.
- Soil nutrient changes.
- Increased erosion risk.
- Altered vegetation succession.

### Important Distinction

Fire is not always ecologically harmful.

- Some ecosystems are adapted to periodic fires.
- Fire can stimulate regeneration in certain species.
- Excessive or unusually intense fires can cause severe ecological damage.

**UPSC Trap:** The ecological impact of fire depends on its frequency, intensity, season and ecosystem characteristics.

---

# 5.15 Deforestation and Forest Degradation

## Deforestation

Conversion of forest land to non-forest land use.

### Major Drivers

- Agricultural expansion.
- Infrastructure development.
- Urbanization.
- Mining.
- Unsustainable land-use conversion.

## Forest Degradation

Reduction in the ecological condition of forests without necessarily converting them to another land use.

### Causes

- Unsustainable timber extraction.
- Repeated fires.
- Excessive grazing.
- Invasive alien species.
- Habitat fragmentation.
- Pollution and other disturbances.

### Comparison

| Deforestation | Forest Degradation |
|---|---|
| Forest converted to another land use | Forest remains but loses ecological quality |
| May result in complete loss of tree cover | Tree cover may remain |
| Direct loss of forest area | Reduction in biodiversity, biomass or ecosystem functions |

---

# 5.16 Afforestation, Reforestation and Restoration

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Afforestation | Establishment of forest on land not recently forested |
| Reforestation | Re-establishment of forest on previously forested land |
| Ecological restoration | Assisting recovery of degraded or damaged ecosystems |
| Assisted Natural Regeneration (ANR) | Supporting natural forest recovery by reducing barriers |
| Social forestry | Tree growing and management to meet community needs |
| Agroforestry | Integration of trees with crops and/or livestock |
| Urban forestry | Establishment and management of trees and forest ecosystems in urban areas |

### Important Facts

- Ecological restoration is broader than tree planting.
- Native species are generally preferable for restoring natural ecosystem composition.
- Plantations are not necessarily equivalent to natural forests.
- Afforestation on natural grasslands can harm grassland ecosystems.

---

# 5.17 Agroforestry and Social Forestry

## Agroforestry

A land-use system integrating trees with agricultural crops and/or livestock.

### Benefits

- Soil conservation.
- Carbon storage.
- Diversified farm income.
- Improved microclimate.
- Reduced erosion.
- Provision of fuelwood, fodder and timber.

### Common Agroforestry Systems

| System | Components |
|---|---|
| Agrisilviculture | Trees + crops |
| Silvopasture | Trees + pasture/livestock |
| Agrosilvopasture | Trees + crops + pasture/livestock |

## Social Forestry

Tree cultivation and forest management intended to meet local community needs.

### Major Forms

- Farm forestry.
- Community forestry.
- Extension forestry.
- Urban forestry.

**Important:** Social forestry and agroforestry overlap but are not identical concepts.

# 5.18 Important Forest Conservation Programmes in India

## A. National Forest Policy, 1988

### Important Objectives

- Environmental stability.
- Maintenance of ecological balance.
- Conservation of natural heritage.
- Prevention of soil erosion.
- Protection of watersheds.
- Meeting local needs for fuelwood, fodder and minor forest produce.

### Important Target

The policy envisages bringing at least **one-third of India's total land area under forest or tree cover**, with a higher aspiration for mountainous and hilly regions.

**Important:** This is a policy objective, not the current actual forest-cover percentage.

---

## B. Joint Forest Management (JFM)

- Formally promoted through a Government of India circular in **1990**.
- Involves cooperation between forest departments and local communities.
- Encourages community participation in forest protection and management.
- Benefit-sharing arrangements vary among states.

**Important:** JFM is a participatory management approach, not a separate statutory protected-area category.

---

## C. Green India Mission

**National Mission for a Green India (GIM)** is one of the missions under the National Action Plan on Climate Change.

### Objectives

- Improve forest and tree cover.
- Enhance ecosystem services.
- Improve biodiversity and carbon sequestration.
- Support forest-dependent livelihoods.

### Important Facts

- NAPCC was launched in **2008**.
- Green India Mission focuses on both forest cover and ecosystem quality.

---

## D. CAMPA

**CAMPA:** Compensatory Afforestation Fund Management and Planning Authority.

### Legal Framework

**Compensatory Afforestation Fund Act, 2016**

### Purpose

To manage and utilize funds collected in connection with the diversion of forest land for non-forest purposes.

### Important Facts

- Provides for National and State Compensatory Afforestation Funds.
- Funds support compensatory afforestation and specified forest-related activities.
- CAMPA funds are not a substitute for avoiding unnecessary forest diversion.

---

# 5.19 Forest Conservation Laws in India

## A. Indian Forest Act, 1927

Provides a legal framework for:

- Reserved forests.
- Protected forests.
- Village forests.
- Regulation of forest produce.
- Certain forest offences.

### Legal Forest Categories

| Category | Important Feature |
|---|---|
| Reserved Forest | Stronger statutory restrictions under the Indian Forest Act |
| Protected Forest | Certain rights may continue subject to statutory restrictions |
| Village Forest | Reserved forest assigned to a village community under applicable provisions |

**Important:** A **protected forest** under the Indian Forest Act is not the same as a **protected area** under the Wild Life (Protection) Act.

---

## B. Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980

The Act regulates diversion of forest land for non-forest purposes.

### 2023 Amendment

The legislation was renamed the:

**Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam, 1980.**

The 2023 amendment modified the Act's scope and introduced specified exemptions and provisions.

### Important Prelims Facts

- Central Government approval is required for specified diversion of forest land.
- The Act regulates forest land use rather than providing the principal legal framework for wildlife sanctuaries and national parks.
- The 2023 amendment changed the statutory framework, including provisions concerning certain land categories and projects.

**Important:** The Act's applicability and exemptions must be read alongside relevant Supreme Court directions and subsequent legal developments.

---

## C. Forest Rights Act, 2006

Full name:

**Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006.**

### Important Features

- Recognizes specified individual and community forest rights.
- Provides for Community Forest Resource rights.
- Recognizes rights related to habitation, livelihood and traditional forest use.
- Assigns an important role to the Gram Sabha.

### Important Distinction

The Forest Rights Act, 2006, is different from the Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980.

| Forest Rights Act, 2006 | Forest Conservation Law, 1980 |
|---|---|
| Recognition of forest-dweller rights | Regulation of forest-land diversion |
| Gram Sabha plays a central role | Central Government approval is important |
| Focuses on rights and forest governance | Focuses on conservation and land-use regulation |

# 5.20 Important Terrestrial Ecosystem Concepts

## A. Ecotone

A transition zone between two ecosystems.

Examples:
- Forest–grassland boundary.
- Forest–desert transition.

## B. Edge Effect

Changes in species composition and ecological conditions at habitat boundaries.

### Important Facts

- Edge habitats may contain species from adjoining ecosystems.
- Habitat fragmentation increases edge-to-interior ratios.
- Increased edge effects may harm forest-interior species.

## C. Ecological Corridors

Landscape connections facilitating movement of organisms between habitat patches.

### Importance

- Maintain gene flow.
- Facilitate seasonal migration.
- Reduce population isolation.
- Support landscape-level conservation.

### Examples

- Elephant corridors.
- Tiger corridors.
- Forest connections between protected areas.
