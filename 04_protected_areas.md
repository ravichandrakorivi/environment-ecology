# Chapter 4: Biodiversity Conservation and Protected Areas
## UPSC Civil Services Preliminary Examination – Study Notes

## 4.1 Biodiversity Conservation: Basic Concepts

**Biodiversity conservation** refers to the protection, management, restoration and sustainable use of biological diversity at genetic, species and ecosystem levels.

### Types of Conservation

Biodiversity conservation is broadly classified into:

1. **In-situ conservation:** Conservation of species within their natural habitats.
2. **Ex-situ conservation:** Conservation of biodiversity outside its natural habitats.

### In-situ vs Ex-situ Conservation

| Feature | In-situ Conservation | Ex-situ Conservation |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Conservation within natural habitats | Conservation outside natural habitats |
| Focus | Species and their ecological interactions | Selected species, populations or genetic material |
| Examples | National parks, wildlife sanctuaries, biosphere reserves | Zoos, botanical gardens, seed banks |
| Advantage | Maintains natural ecological and evolutionary processes | Supports captive breeding, research and genetic preservation |
| Limitation | Species remain exposed to some habitat-level threats | Cannot fully reproduce natural ecosystems |

**Important:** Ex-situ conservation complements in-situ conservation; it does not replace habitat protection.

### Examples of Ex-situ Conservation

| Method | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Zoological gardens | Conservation and captive breeding of animals |
| Botanical gardens | Conservation of living plant collections |
| Seed banks | Long-term storage of seeds |
| Gene banks | Conservation of genetic material |
| Cryopreservation | Storage of biological material at extremely low temperatures |
| Tissue culture | Propagation and preservation of plant material |
| Captive breeding | Increasing populations of threatened species under managed conditions |

# 4.2 Protected Areas in India

The principal statutory protected-area categories under the **Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972** are:

1. Wildlife Sanctuaries.
2. National Parks.
3. Conservation Reserves.
4. Community Reserves.

**Important:** Tiger reserves are also recognized under the Act through separate provisions, while biosphere reserves are not a separate statutory protected-area category under the Act.

## Comparison of Protected Areas

| Feature | National Park | Wildlife Sanctuary | Conservation Reserve | Community Reserve |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Principal legal provisions | Sections 35–38 | Sections 18–34 | Sections 36A–36B | Sections 36C–36D |
| General purpose | Protect wildlife and ecosystems | Protect wildlife and habitats | Conserve landscapes, habitats and corridors | Community-based conservation |
| Land | Government-controlled protected land following prescribed rights settlement | Land subject to statutory protection and rights provisions | Government-owned land | Private or community land |
| Human activities | More restrictive | Certain regulated activities and rights may continue | Managed under conservation-reserve provisions | Managed with community participation |
| Declaration | State Government; Central Government has powers in specified circumstances | State Government; Central Government has powers in specified circumstances | State Government | State Government |
| Wildlife protection | Strong statutory protection | Strong statutory protection | Statutory conservation protection | Statutory conservation protection |

### Important Distinctions

- A national park generally has a **more restrictive legal regime** than a wildlife sanctuary.
- Wildlife sanctuaries are not automatically open to unrestricted human activities.
- Conservation reserves are associated with **government-owned land**.
- Community reserves are associated with **private or community land**.
- National parks and wildlife sanctuaries are established under the same principal wildlife legislation.

# 4.3 National Parks

### Definition

A **National Park** is a protected area declared under Section 35 of the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972, for protecting wildlife, habitats and ecological features.

### Important Legal Features

- The State Government may initiate the process of declaring a national park.
- Rights over land are settled or acquired according to the Act before final notification.
- Grazing of livestock is prohibited in national parks, subject to the Act's specific provisions.
- Destruction, exploitation or removal of wildlife and habitat is generally prohibited except under statutory authorization.
- Alteration of national park boundaries requires a recommendation of the **National Board for Wildlife**.

### Important Facts

- **Jim Corbett National Park**, Uttarakhand, was established in **1936** as Hailey National Park.
- It is generally recognized as India's first national park.
- National parks may protect terrestrial, freshwater or marine ecosystems.

# 4.4 Wildlife Sanctuaries

### Definition

A **Wildlife Sanctuary** is an area notified under the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972, for protecting wildlife and its habitat.

### Important Features

- Certain existing rights may continue subject to the Act.
- Entry and activities are regulated.
- Hunting is generally prohibited except under specified statutory exceptions.
- Grazing may be regulated, unlike the general prohibition applicable to national parks.
- Alteration of sanctuary boundaries requires a recommendation of the National Board for Wildlife.

# 4.5 Conservation Reserves and Community Reserves

Both categories were introduced through the **Wild Life (Protection) Amendment Act, 2002**.

## A. Conservation Reserves

**Section 36A**

The State Government may declare government-owned land as a conservation reserve, particularly areas adjacent to national parks and sanctuaries or linking protected areas.

### Important Features

- Established on government-owned land.
- May protect wildlife corridors.
- Managed with local participation through a Conservation Reserve Management Committee.
- Intended to improve landscape-level conservation.

## B. Community Reserves

**Section 36C**

The State Government may declare private or community land as a community reserve when a community or individual volunteers to conserve wildlife and habitats.

### Important Features

- Established on private or community land.
- Requires voluntary conservation participation.
- Managed through a Community Reserve Management Committee.
- Land-use changes are subject to statutory restrictions.

### Key Difference

| Conservation Reserve | Community Reserve |
|---|---|
| Government-owned land | Private or community land |
| Often protects corridors and adjoining habitats | Encourages voluntary community conservation |
| Section 36A | Section 36C |

# 4.6 Biosphere Reserves

### Definition

A **Biosphere Reserve** is a large landscape or seascape designated to promote biodiversity conservation, sustainable development and research.

The concept is associated with UNESCO's **Man and the Biosphere (MAB) Programme**, launched in **1971**.

### Important Distinction

- National parks and wildlife sanctuaries are statutory protected areas under Indian law.
- Biosphere reserves are not a separate statutory category under the Wild Life (Protection) Act.
- A biosphere reserve may contain national parks, wildlife sanctuaries, settlements and other land-use areas.

## Zonation of Biosphere Reserves

| Zone | Main Purpose | Human Activities |
|---|---|---|
| **Core** | Strict biodiversity conservation | Highly restricted |
| **Buffer** | Research, monitoring, education and compatible activities | Regulated |
| **Transition** | Sustainable economic and human activities | Settlements and sustainable resource use |

### Important Facts

- The **core zone** receives the strongest protection.
- The **buffer zone** surrounds or adjoins the core.
- The **transition zone** supports sustainable development.
- UNESCO recognition and national designation are different processes.

# 4.11 Eco-Sensitive Zones (ESZs)

### Definition

Eco-Sensitive Zones are areas notified around certain protected areas to regulate activities that may adversely affect their ecosystems.

### Legal Basis

- Notified under the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986** and the Environment (Protection) Rules, 1986.
- Notifications are issued by the Central Government through the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change.
- Their purpose is to provide a transition between protected ecosystems and surrounding human-dominated landscapes.

### Classification of Activities

| Prohibited | Regulated | Generally Permitted |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial mining and quarrying, subject to applicable legal directions | Construction activities | Agriculture and horticulture |
| Establishment of highly polluting industries | Tourism-related activities | Rainwater harvesting |
| Other activities prohibited by the relevant ESZ notification | Infrastructure development | Organic farming |

**Important:** The precise classification depends on the applicable ESZ notification and legal directions.

### Supreme Court Judgment, 2023

In *T. N. Godavarman Thirumulpad v. Union of India*, the Supreme Court clarified that:

- A **uniform one-kilometre ESZ** cannot be imposed around every national park and wildlife sanctuary.
- ESZ boundaries must be determined according to the circumstances of individual protected areas.
- Existing draft and final ESZ notifications and proposals received by the Ministry were addressed through specific exceptions.
- Mining within national parks, wildlife sanctuaries and within one kilometre of their boundaries is prohibited under the Court's directions. [National Digital Library of India](https://ndl.iitkgp.ac.in/lw_document/supreme_court_india/supreme_court/32334?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**UPSC Prelims Trap:** It is incorrect to state that every national park and wildlife sanctuary must have an ESZ of exactly one kilometre.

# 4.12 Sacred Groves

Sacred groves are patches of natural vegetation protected through traditional religious, cultural or community practices.

### Important Features

- Examples of **community-based in-situ conservation**.
- Often protect native vegetation and associated fauna.
- May conserve rare and endemic species.
- Their protection is based primarily on traditional customs, although some also receive statutory protection.

# 4.13 Biodiversity Heritage Sites

### Legal Basis

**Section 37 of the Biological Diversity Act, 2002**, as amended.

### Important Features

- State Governments may notify areas of biodiversity importance as Biodiversity Heritage Sites in accordance with the Act.
- The process involves consultation with local bodies and recommendations of the State Biodiversity Board.
- Sites may contain important, unique, fragile or threatened biodiversity.
- They may occur outside national parks and wildlife sanctuaries.

### Important Example

**Nallur Tamarind Grove, Karnataka**
- Recognized as India's first Biodiversity Heritage Site in **2007**.
- Known for its old tamarind trees.

### Biodiversity Heritage Site vs Biosphere Reserve

| Biodiversity Heritage Site | Biosphere Reserve |
|---|---|
| Recognized under the Biological Diversity Act | Designated under India's biosphere reserve programme |
| Notified by State Government | Designated through national administrative processes |
| May be a relatively small area | Generally covers a large landscape or seascape |
| Focuses on biodiversity of special significance | Combines conservation, research and sustainable development |

# 4.14 IUCN Protected Area Management Categories

The IUCN classifies protected areas according to their **management objectives**.

| IUCN Category | Name |
|---|---|
| Ia | Strict Nature Reserve |
| Ib | Wilderness Area |
| II | National Park |
| III | Natural Monument or Feature |
| IV | Habitat/Species Management Area |
| V | Protected Landscape/Seascape |
| VI | Protected Area with Sustainable Use of Natural Resources |

### Important Distinctions

- IUCN categories are **international management classifications**.
- They are not the same as India's statutory protected-area categories.
- An Indian national park is not automatically assigned IUCN Category II merely because it is called a national park.
- IUCN protected-area categories are different from IUCN Red List categories.

# 4.16 Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972 — Important Provisions

The Act provides the principal legal framework for wildlife protection and protected areas in India.

### Important Amendments

| Amendment | Important Provisions |
|---|---|
| 1991 | Strengthened wildlife protection; provisions relating to specified plants and zoos |
| 2002 | Introduced conservation reserves and community reserves |
| 2006 | Established the statutory framework for NTCA and WCCB |

## Schedules After the 2022 Amendment

The amended Act has **four schedules**, effective from **1 April 2023**.

| Schedule | Subject |
|---|---|

| **Schedule III** | Specified protected plants |

# 4.17 Protected Areas: Important Comparisons

| Feature | National Park | Wildlife Sanctuary | Biosphere Reserve | Tiger Reserve |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Legal status | Statutory protected area | Statutory protected area | Administrative conservation designation | Statutory tiger-conservation framework |
| Main objective | Wildlife and ecosystem protection | Wildlife and habitat protection | Conservation and sustainable development | Tiger and habitat conservation |
| Human activities | Highly restricted | Regulated | Vary across zones | Vary between core and buffer |
| May include settlements? | Generally restricted following rights settlement | Subject to statutory rights and regulations | Yes, especially transition zone | Possible in buffer areas |
| Relevant authority | State/Central Government as provided by law | State/Central Government as provided by law | MoEFCC and State Governments | NTCA and State Governments |

### Key UPSC Distinctions

**1. National Park vs Wildlife Sanctuary**

National parks generally impose stricter restrictions on grazing and private rights.

**2. Biosphere Reserve vs National Park**

A biosphere reserve can contain one or more national parks, wildlife sanctuaries and inhabited areas.

**3. Tiger Reserve vs National Park**

A tiger reserve may include a national park, but the two are not identical legal designations.

**4. Elephant Reserve vs Tiger Reserve**

Tiger reserves have a specific statutory framework; elephant reserves are designated under Project Elephant.

**5. Conservation Reserve vs Community Reserve**

Government-owned land versus private/community land.
