## Conservation International (CI)

Conservation International (CI) is an international non-governmental organisation (NGO) working to protect biodiversity, conserve natural ecosystems and promote sustainable development.

### 1. Basic Facts

| Feature               | Details                                                                     |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Established           | 1987                                                                        |
| Headquarters          | Arlington, Virginia, USA                                                    |
| Nature                | International non-governmental organisation (NGO)                           |
| Primary focus         | Biodiversity conservation, ecosystem protection and sustainable development |
| Important association | Identification and conservation of biodiversity hotspots                    |

### 2. Role in Biodiversity Hotspots

The biodiversity hotspot concept was proposed by Norman Myers in 1988 and was subsequently developed and promoted by Conservation International.

To qualify as a biodiversity hotspot, a region must satisfy both conditions:

1. It must contain at least 1,500 endemic vascular plant species.
2. It must have lost at least 70% of its original natural vegetation.

These criteria identify regions with exceptional plant endemism that have experienced severe habitat loss.

## Wildlife Institute of India (WII)

The Wildlife Institute of India (WII) is a premier national institution for wildlife research, training and conservation in India.

| Feature                 | Details                                                                  |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |

| Headquarters            | Dehradun, Uttarakhand                                                    |
| Administrative Ministry | Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC)              |

| Primary functions       | Wildlife research, conservation planning, training and capacity building |
| Official website        | [https://wii.gov.in](https://wii.gov.in?utm_source=chatgpt.com)          |

### Major Functions

- Conducts scientific research on wildlife, biodiversity and habitats.
- Provides specialised training in wildlife management to forest and wildlife professionals.
- Supports wildlife population assessments and conservation programmes.
- Conducts research on endangered species, protected areas and wildlife corridors.
- Provides scientific and technical advice to government agencies.

### Important UPSC Prelims Distinctions

- WII: Wildlife research, training and conservation science.
- Zoological Survey of India (ZSI): Faunal survey, taxonomy and documentation.
- Botanical Survey of India (BSI): Floristic survey, taxonomy and documentation.
- National Biodiversity Authority (NBA): Statutory authority established under the Biological Diversity Act, 2002.

Remember: WII is an autonomous institution, not a statutory regulatory authority such as the National Biodiversity Authority.

## National Biodiversity Authority (NBA)

The National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) is a statutory body established under the Biological Diversity Act, 2002, for implementing provisions relating to biodiversity conservation, sustainable use and fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising from biological resources.

### 1. Basic Facts

| Feature                 | Details                                                     |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Established             | 2003                                                        |
| Governing legislation   | Biological Diversity Act, 2002                              |
| Nature                  | Statutory body                                              |
| Headquarters            | Chennai, Tamil Nadu                                         |
| Administrative ministry | Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC) |
| International framework | Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD), 1992              |

### 2. Major Functions

- Regulates specified access to India's biological resources and associated traditional knowledge.
- Advises the Central Government on biodiversity conservation, sustainable use and benefit-sharing.
- Facilitates Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS) arrangements.
- Advises State Governments on identifying areas of biodiversity importance for notification as Biodiversity Heritage Sites.
- Performs functions relating to intellectual property rights involving Indian biological resources.

### 3. Three-Tier Institutional Structure

| Level    | Institution                                                                      | Main Responsibility                                                                    |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| National | National Biodiversity Authority (NBA)                                            | National-level regulation, approvals and advice                                        |
| State    | State Biodiversity Boards (SBBs) / Union Territory Biodiversity Councils (UTBCs) | State/UT-level regulation and conservation advice                                      |
| Local    | Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs)                                        | Conservation, documentation of local biodiversity and associated traditional knowledge |

BMCs are also associated with preparing People's Biodiversity Registers (PBRs), which document local biological resources and traditional knowledge.&#x20;

## 9.1 Constitutional Provisions for Environmental Protection

The Constitution of India contains provisions relating to environmental protection, forests, wildlife and public health.

### Important Constitutional Articles

| Article | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Article 21** | Right to life and personal liberty; judicially interpreted to include the right to a clean and healthy environment |
| **Article 48A** | Directive Principle requiring the State to endeavour to protect and improve the environment and safeguard forests and wildlife |
| **Article 51A(g)** | Fundamental duty of citizens to protect and improve the natural environment and have compassion for living creatures |
| **Article 47** | Directive Principle concerning nutrition, standard of living and public health |
| **Article 32** | Constitutional remedies before the Supreme Court for enforcement of fundamental rights |
| **Article 226** | High Courts' power to issue writs |
| **Article 253** | Parliament's power to make laws implementing international agreements |

### 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976

Introduced:

- **Article 48A:** Environmental protection as a Directive Principle of State Policy.
- **Article 51A(g):** Environmental protection as a Fundamental Duty.

It also transferred **forests and protection of wild animals and birds** from the State List to the Concurrent List.

### Seventh Schedule

| Entry | Subject | List |
|---|---|---|
| Entry 17A | Forests | Concurrent List |
| Entry 17B | Protection of wild animals and birds | Concurrent List |
| Entry 6 | Public health and sanitation | State List |
| Entry 17 | Water, subject to Entry 56 of the Union List | State List |
| Entry 56 | Regulation and development of inter-State rivers and river valleys under specified conditions | Union List |

**UPSC Prelims Trap:** Forests and protection of wild animals and birds are subjects in the **Concurrent List**, not exclusively the State List.

# 9.3 Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972

- Protect wild animals, birds and specified plants.
- Regulate hunting and wildlife-related activities.

- Regulate wildlife trade.
- Provide for wildlife conservation institutions.

| **Schedule II** | Other protected animals |
| **Schedule III** | Specified protected plants |

# 9.7 Forest Conservation Legislation

## Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980

The legislation regulates specified activities involving forest land, including diversion for non-forest purposes.

### Important Features

- Requires prior approval of the Central Government for specified forest-land diversion.
- Regulates dereservation and non-forest use of forest land.
- Provides a legal framework for forest conservation.

## 2023 Amendment

Renamed the legislation:

**Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam, 1980.**

### Important Changes

- Modified the statutory scope of land covered by the Act.
- Introduced provisions concerning specified land categories and projects.
- Provided exemptions subject to statutory conditions.

### Important Judicial Context

The Supreme Court's **T. N. Godavarman Thirumulpad** litigation has played an important role in determining the interpretation and protection of forests.

In interim proceedings following the 2023 amendment, the Supreme Court directed continued adherence to the broader understanding of forests pending further steps concerning identification and records.

**Important:** For questions on the current applicability of the Act, distinguish the statutory text from operative judicial directions.

# 9.8 Forest Rights Act, 2006

Full name:

**Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006.**

### Objectives

- Recognize specified historical forest rights.
- Address historical injustices affecting forest-dwelling communities.
- Provide for individual and community forest rights.
- Recognize community participation in forest governance.

### Important Features

- Gram Sabha plays a central role in initiating the rights-recognition process.
- Recognizes Community Forest Resource rights.
- Covers eligible forest-dwelling Scheduled Tribes and other traditional forest dwellers.

### Eligibility Distinction

| Category | Important Requirement |
|---|---|
| Forest-dwelling Scheduled Tribes | Must satisfy the Act's residence and dependence requirements |
| Other Traditional Forest Dwellers | Must satisfy the requirement of residence and dependence for at least three generations before 13 December 2005 |

**One generation = 25 years** under the Act.

Thus, three generations correspond to **75 years**.

### Important Distinction

The Forest Rights Act concerns recognition of rights, whereas forest conservation legislation principally regulates forest-land diversion.

# 9.12 Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA)

**Environmental Impact Assessment** is a process for identifying and evaluating the likely environmental consequences of a proposed project before a decision is taken.

### Legal Framework

- Environment (Protection) Act, 1986.
- EIA Notification, 2006, as amended.

### Important Stages

| Stage | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Screening | Determines whether specified projects require EIA |
| Scoping | Identifies environmental issues to be studied |
| Public consultation | Obtains views of affected persons and other stakeholders |
| Appraisal | Expert assessment of the proposal and environmental implications |

### Project Categories

| Category | Appraisal Authority |
|---|---|
| Category A | Central Government through MoEFCC |
| Category B | State Environment Impact Assessment Authority (SEIAA), subject to applicable provisions |

**Important:** Category B projects may be subdivided into **B1 and B2**. The applicable EIA and public-consultation requirements differ.

### Important Institutions

- **EAC:** Expert Appraisal Committee.
- **SEIAA:** State Environment Impact Assessment Authority.
- **SEAC:** State Expert Appraisal Committee.

**UPSC Trap:** Environmental clearance and forest clearance are distinct regulatory approvals. Depending on the project, both may be necessary.

# 9.18 Important Environmental Principles

| Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sustainable Development | Meeting present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet theirs |
| Precautionary Principle | Lack of full scientific certainty should not justify postponing appropriate measures against serious environmental harm |
| Polluter Pays Principle | Polluter should bear the costs of pollution prevention, control and remediation |
| Public Trust Doctrine | Certain natural resources are held by the State in trust for public use and benefit |
| Intergenerational Equity | Environmental resources should be conserved with regard to future generations |
| Environmental Justice | Fair distribution of environmental benefits, burdens and decision-making opportunities |

### Important Judicial Associations

| Case | Important Principle or Issue |
|---|---|
| **Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum v. Union of India (1996)** | Precautionary Principle and Polluter Pays Principle |
| **M. C. Mehta v. Union of India (Oleum Gas Leak Case, 1986–87)** | Absolute liability for hazardous industries |
| **Indian Council for Enviro-Legal Action v. Union of India (1996)** | Polluter Pays Principle |
| **M. C. Mehta v. Kamal Nath (1996)** | Public Trust Doctrine |
| **T. N. Godavarman Thirumulpad v. Union of India** | Forest protection and interpretation of forest-related law |

### Absolute Liability vs Strict Liability

| Absolute Liability | Strict Liability |
|---|---|
| Developed in Indian environmental jurisprudence in the Oleum Gas Leak case | Associated with *Rylands v. Fletcher* |
| Applies to enterprises engaged in hazardous or inherently dangerous activities under the judicial doctrine | Traditional common-law doctrine with recognized exceptions |
| Does not permit the traditional exceptions available under strict liability | Recognizes specified exceptions |
